FOMC Review: Fed Expected to Maintain Tight Stance Until 2% PCE Target Is Met

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 3 min read

Source: 서울경제TV · CPI 내려왔다고 안심했는데...연준이 보는 물가는 따로 있었습니다?. Summarised from that video.

Analysis of FOMC Meeting Results and Fed's Inflation Metrics

In a broadcast analyzing the results of the recent FOMC meeting, an analyst explained the key inflation metrics emphasized by the U.S. Federal Reserve (Fed) and the direction of future monetary policy. The analyst pointed out that while the Consumer Price Index (CPI) has shown a downward trend from a peak of 9.1%, the metric that the Fed considers more important in actual policy decisions is the Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) price index.

The reason the Fed places greater emphasis on the PCE metric is that its scope of investigation is broader than that of the CPI. The analyst explained that because the PCE price index has not fallen as sharply as the CPI, it is difficult to conclude that inflationary pressures have been fully resolved.

Interest Rate Hikes and Maintaining Monetary Tightening

The analyst stressed that adjustments according to the economic cycle and precise execution of interest rate policy are necessary to firmly contain inflation, noting the Fed's core target.

시의 적절하게 2%로 돌리겠다.

"We will bring it back to 2% in a timely manner."

The analyst anticipated that the Fed will maintain its monetary tightening stance until it achieves the 2.0% PCE target. With the inflation rate projected to be around 3.4% at the end of this year, the analysis suggests that additional interest rate hikes are inevitable to reach the 2% target.

Dot Plot Outlook and Member Trends

At this FOMC meeting, all 12 voting members were shown to have supported the rate hike. While the dot plot presented projections of about one additional rate hike this year and a pause through next year, the analyst also noted the possibility of further hikes depending on market conditions.

In conclusion, the Fed considers curbing inflation its top priority and is expected to continue its tightening stance until the PCE metric returns to the target of 2%.
