Prolonged Blackouts and 40-Degree Heatwave Bring Pakistan to a Standstill as Electricity Theft Soars

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 4 min read

Source: KBS 세계는지금 · [ENG SUB] 40°C Heat & No Power! 멈춰 선 파키스탄, 전기 도둑까지 극성 (KBS_466회_2026.09.05.방송). Summarised from that video.

Karachi, Pakistan's largest commercial city, has fallen silent in broad daylight. As the power supply crisis has worsened, numerous shops across the city have closed their doors, leaving darkness inside the non-operational stores.

Shops and Hospitals Grounded in Heatwave: Blackouts Disrupt Daily Life

At an electronics store in downtown Karachi, the lights are off and customers are nowhere to be seen. With electricity supply failing to keep up with demand, targeted power cuts known as 'load shedding' are being implemented, leaving areas without electricity for 14 to 15 hours a day.

Dairy shops have also suffered heavy losses as milk spoiled due to the blackouts, and patients with heat-related illnesses are surging amid scorching heat hovering around 40 degrees Celsius. However, even the hospitals tasked with treating them could not escape the power crisis. At a general hospital in the city, corridors remain dark and beds empty even during daytime hours, and cases of power cuts occurring during surgery have also taken place.

Electricity Bill Burdens and Rampant 'Electricity Theft'

Residents unable to use electricity in the sweltering heat head to ice stalls, packing ice into plastic bags to endure the heat. Inside homes, a small bulb is powered by a battery, but it turns off after 30 minutes at most, and even fans cannot operate, leaving residents suffering in unventilated, steaming heat.

Furthermore, residential electricity rates in Pakistan range from 7.74 rupees to over 47 rupees per kWh depending on usage, posing a major burden for low-income households. Another major cause of the power crisis is cited as 'electricity theft,' where electricity is siphoned off by illegally connecting wires to power poles. The annual loss from this alone reaches approximately 2.8 trillion won in Korean currency.

Authorities have deployed police for 'Operation Kundo Removal' to tear down illegal wiring, but electricity theft persists as residents resist with assaults on crackdown teams and hostage situations.

Lack of Transmission Infrastructure and Chronic Structural Issues

With the government unable to resolve the power shortage, a growing number of residents are taking self-reliant measures by installing solar panels at their own expense to generate electricity directly.

We have to manage all things by our own because the government don't take care of normal people. They don't want to do anything new for the new generation.

"Since the government does not guarantee the basic rights of ordinary citizens, we have to find ways to survive on our own. The government is doing nothing for the new generation being born and growing up."

According to expert analysis, Pakistan's total power generation capacity has a surplus power of 10 to 12 GW, meaning nationwide power production itself exceeds demand. However, a critical shortage and instability in transmission network infrastructure to send generated electricity to where it is needed is pointed out as the root cause of the chronic power crisis.
