Presidential Approval Rebounds as Real Estate, Stock Market, and Parliamentary Audit Pose Key Political Variables

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 3 min read

Source: MBN News · [시사스페셜] 이 대통령 지지율, 하락 끝내고 '반등'?…향후 변수는?. Summarised from that video.

As the president's job performance approval rating stopped falling and showed a slight rebound, real estate issues, stock market volatility, and issues surrounding the second half of the parliamentary audit have emerged as major variables in the political landscape.

Slight Rebound in Presidential Approval... "Recovery of Core Support and Building Trust Cited as Tasks"

Kim Mi-hyun, head of R&Search, evaluated that recent public opinion polls showed the decline in presidential approval ratings halted and exhibited a slight upward trend. However, she suggested that restoring trust and preparing proactive measures are necessary for this to lead to a full recovery among core supporters, including those in their 40s and 50s and white-collar workers.

Real Estate and Stock Market Instability Affect Public Sentiment... "Effective Policy Response Needed"

Bae Jong-chan, head of Insight K, cited the real estate and stock markets as primary factors decisively influencing public sentiment in the Seoul metropolitan area and among young people. He stated that housing supply stabilization measures to address loan regulations and lease market anxiety are urgently needed, alongside active stock market boost policies such as improving corporate governance and expanding shareholder dividends.

Gap in Party Approval Ratings Narrows... Aftermath of Internal Conflicts and Security Issues

Experts appearing on broadcasts pointed to internal conflicts within ruling and opposition parties and security anxiety factors as reasons for the narrowing gap in party approval ratings. They emphasized that to prevent the departure of moderates and young people, parties must demonstrate policy competitiveness and internal harmony rather than relying on windfall benefits.

Confrontation in Second Half of Parliamentary Audit... Clashes Over Witness Attendance and Current Issues Expected to Intensify

In the second half of the parliamentary audit, which has passed its midpoint, confrontation between the ruling and opposition parties surrounding audits of key presidential secretariats and agencies, as well as witness attendance, is expected to become even more intense. Director Kim noted the need for a proactive response to current issues, while Director Bae analyzed that presenting logical and systematic results of state affairs management will determine approval ratings.
