"No Choice Even if We Risk Our Lives": Working Poor Around the World Endure Dangerous Jobs for Survival

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 5 min read

Source: KBS 세계는지금 · 지구촌 서민, 그 고달픈 삶 (KBS_2026.07.25.방송). Summarised from that video.

In many parts of the world, ordinary people are driven to work in extremely dangerous and poor environments without safety equipment just to make a living. From landfills and sulfur mines in Indonesia to sewers in Pakistan and tanneries in Bangladesh, their workplaces are constantly exposed to deadly toxic gases, pollution, and the risk of accidents.

Asia's Largest Open-Air Dump, Bantar Gebang

Bantar Gebang, located on the outskirts of Jakarta, the capital of Indonesia, is the world's largest open-air landfill, with trash piled up as high as a 17-story apartment building. The amount of waste coming into this site reaches about 7,000 tons a day, which is three times the daily output of Seoul.

About 6,000 residents make a living by collecting recyclables from the pouring piles of garbage. Operating right next to heavy machinery running in real time, workers carrying carts and baskets face a high risk of accidents, while the collapse of garbage piles and soil and groundwater contamination caused by leachate pose severe problems.

Meskipun kita berat ini berdempetan sama alat berat, mau diapa-apain lagi kan?

"Even if we have to take risks, we have no choice but to rummage through the trash. We don't have any other option."

Pakistan's Sewer Cleaners Entering 60cm Manholes

In Karachi, Pakistan, sanitation workers climb down narrow manholes measuring around 60 centimeters in diameter to clean sewers. Although these are confined spaces filled with toxic gases that carry a high risk of suffocation, the only safety gear provided to workers consists of a safety belt tied around their body and a rope.

Workers feel their way through sewage with bare hands and bare feet, scooping up filth while cutting their hands on broken glass or sharp debris. This type of sanitation work is mainly performed by Christians, a religious minority in Pakistan—a result stemming from discriminatory employment structures and social perceptions originating during British colonial rule.

Bangladesh Tanneries Defenselessly Exposed to Toxic Chemicals

In the Savar industrial zone near Dhaka, the capital of Bangladesh, around 170 tanneries are clustered together. While the leather industry is a major sector generating more than $1 billion in annual exports for Bangladesh, about 200 toxic chemicals, including hydrochloric acid, formic acid, and chromium, are used during the processing phase.

Most workers handle toxic chemicals with bare hands and bare feet without protective gear, suffering from skin erosion and respiratory illnesses. In unventilated factories, even children are forced into long hours of labor without protective equipment, and cases where workers fail to receive proper wages under the pretext of learning practical skills are frequent.

এই কেমিক্যালের কারণে এই চামড়া শেষ হইলে ঠিক হয়ে যায়।

"My hands have eroded because of the chemicals. The pain disappears only after work is done."

Gaza Strip Turned into Massive Garbage Mountains After War

In the center of Gaza City, the largest city in the Palestinian Gaza Strip, a massive pile of garbage has formed after being left unattended for two years since the outbreak of war. As most residents who lost their homes due to Israeli bombings live in tent cities, the public health crisis has deepened as flies and mosquitoes surge around the neglected trash.

When it rains, pollutants in the garbage wash in with rainwater, causing water pollution, threatening the residents' living environment as water in storage facilities turns brown.

Battle with Sulfur Gas: Mount Ijen in Indonesia

A massive sulfur mine is located inside the crater of Mount Ijen in East Java, Indonesia. Miners extract sulfur in a traditional manner amid high-temperature toxic gases containing sulfur dioxide and hydrogen sulfide. Inhaled sulfur dioxide causes acute respiratory illnesses and mucous membrane damage, but with no company-issued masks, workers rely on face coverings bought out of their own pockets.

Physical damage accumulates over time, causing teeth to corrode and fall out from the gas toxicity, or leaving burn marks on their hands. Recently, miners unable to endure the sulfur gas have turned to pulling human-powered carts to transport tourists to the mountain summit, but the grueling labor of climbing a steep 3 km mountain trail continues.

Unimproved and Poor Working Conditions

Underprivileged workers across the globe continue to risk their lives working amid poverty, social discrimination, and the absence of institutional safety measures. Attention and policy support from the international community and national governments are urgently needed to improve the working conditions of those left behind behind industrial prosperity and urban sanitation.
