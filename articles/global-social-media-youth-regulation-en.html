'Social Media Addiction' Endangers Youth Mental Health: Countries Worldwide Move Fast to Enforce Age Restrictions and Big Tech Accountability

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 4 min read

Source: JTBC News · 세계는 지금 '청소년 안티 SNS' 도입 중…한국도 법제화 시동 [이슈PLAY] /JTBC News. Summarised from that video.

As concerns grow over excessive social media use threatening youth mental health, governments and judiciaries around the world are taking strong regulatory action. Rulings holding Big Tech companies legally responsible for addictive platform design continue to emerge, while legislation banning social media use by age group is also spreading.

Big Tech Held Accountable for Addiction: Massive Damages and Fines in the US

The US judiciary ruled that social media platforms were aware of the negative impact on youth mental health but left it unaddressed. Courts including the Los Angeles Court and a New Mexico jury found Big Tech companies such as Meta and Google guilty of addicting young users and exposing them to harmful content, imposing large compensation payments and fines.

California Attorney General Rob Bonta strongly highlighted Meta's responsibility regarding the lawsuit.

Meta is accountable for Meta. What it knew, what it didn't do knowing that, and how it lied to the public.

"Meta is accountable for Meta. What it knew, what it didn't do knowing that, and how it lied to the public."

'Social Media Banning for Minors' Spreads to Europe, Asia, and Beyond

National-level age-limit regulations are also being introduced one after another. Australia implemented legislation restricting youth under 16 from creating and maintaining social media accounts, while the French Parliament also passed a bill banning social media use for minors under 15. The UK, the European Union (EU), Indonesia, and Malaysia are also pushing to mandate safety measures such as age-specific usage restrictions, blocking late-night access, and banning auto-play.

South Korean Government Seeks Account Restrictions for Under-14s and Algorithm Regulations

The South Korean government is also reviewing institutional measures to prevent youth over-immersion in social media. The Broadcasting Media and Communications Commission is exploring measures to limit social media account creation for adolescents under 14, and gradually restrict recommendation algorithms and auto-play features that induce over-immersion for youth aged 14 to under 19.

Lee Seong-yeop, director of the Technology Law and Policy Center at Korea University, emphasized the need to introduce proactive government regulations.

인간의 존엄성을 가지고 존재의 의미를 가지고 살기 위해서는 최소한으로 필요한 조치들이다. 국가가 SNS나 AI의 부작용에 대해 정책적 조치를 취하고 따르도록 선언하는 의미에서도 중요하다.

"These are the minimum necessary measures required to live with human dignity and a sense of existence. They are also important in the sense that the state declares policy measures regarding the side effects of social media or AI and requires compliance."

Ensuring Effectiveness and Platform Structural Reform Remain Challenges

However, concerns are also being raised over the effectiveness of age limit measures. Analysis of precedent cases such as Australia showed that while account ownership rates decreased, research findings revealed that the actual reduction in usage rates was minimal due to loopholes such as VPN circumvention and false age entry. Experts advise that beyond simply blocking by age, platforms must improve their algorithm designs that induce excessive immersion and establish strict age verification systems.
