"Great Opportunities in Central Asian Market": Kim Sang-shin, Owner of Korean Restaurant in Almaty, Kazakhstan

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 5 min read

Source: 세계속한국인 · 예금이자만 18%인 카자흐스탄에서 한식당 운영해, 하루 매출 400만원 하는 한국인 부부의 하루____________________해외판 [휴먼스토리,인터뷰,인간극장]. Summarised from that video.

Kim Sang-shin (김상신), CEO of Korean restaurant 'Juane' in Almaty, Kazakhstan, visits the large wholesale market 'Altyn Orda' every week to buy ingredients. Kim entered Kazakhstan in January 2008 and has been running his business locally for a long time.

At local wholesale markets, merchants often ask Asians if they are Chinese first. Kim explained that this is due to the high proportion of Chinese people in the local market.

"너 중국 사람이냐라는 질문을 해요. 그만큼 중국 애들이 많다는 거죠. 우리는 한국 사람이라고 그러면 미소로 확 변해요."

"They ask, 'Are you Chinese?' That's how many Chinese people there are here. But when we say we are Korean, their expressions immediately turn into warm smiles."

Prices and Entrepreneurial Environment in Kazakhstan

In Kazakhstan, general prices in downtown areas are similar to those in South Korea, but ingredients such as agricultural produce are relatively cheap when purchased at wholesale markets. Regarding the financial environment, bank deposit interest rates are high at around 18% per year.

In terms of startup conditions, concepts such as key money or security deposits are not commonly used, providing advantages when choosing a location. The restaurant Kim operates is about 50 pyeong (around 70 seats) inside a luxury apartment complex, with a monthly rent of around 2 million tenge (approximately 6 million Korean won). Starting a new restaurant from scratch without any existing facilities costs more than 100,000 USD.

Operating Korean Food Adhering to Traditional Methods

Kim, who previously worked in the butchery and imported meat distribution business in Korea, sticks to traditional Korean flavors rather than localized dishes. He personally selects meat for butchering and prepares basic side dishes—including kimchi, traditional pastes, and mung bean sprouts—fresh daily for customers.

Kim assessed that while preference for Korean culture and Korean food is very high locally, there is still a shortage of establishments offering authentic Korean flavors.

Advice for Young People Considering Going Overseas

Kazakhstan has a system where one can enter on a business visa and apply for permanent residency after completing certain procedures. Kim advised young people to escape high domestic real estate prices and fierce competition, and instead look toward overseas markets.

"한국에 국한돼 있지 말고 지금 글로벌 시대에 도전해 볼 만합니다. 우리가 살아온 시대를 여기는 살고 있기 때문에 훨씬 창업하기가 수월합니다."

"Don't limit yourself to Korea; in today's global era, it's worth taking on the challenge. Since this place is living through an era we have already experienced, starting a business here is much easier."
