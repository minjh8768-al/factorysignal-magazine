Crypto Price Manipulation Yields 1.4 Billion Won per Case: Authorities Outline 3 Major Schemes and 4 Market Vulnerabilities

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 5 min read

Source: 경제학 사용설명서 · 코인 시세조종 '경주마·가두리·대형고래' — 금융당국이 붙인 이름의 정체. Summarised from that video.

Average Unfair Gain of 1.4 Billion Won Per Price Manipulation Case

The Financial Services Commission and the Financial Supervisory Service disclosed the results of an investigation into unfair trading in the virtual asset market conducted since the enforcement of the Virtual Asset User Protection Act. Investigations were completed for about 40 cases, of which around 30 were referred or notified to investigative authorities. A total of 25 suspects were identified.

The average illegal profit per price manipulation case confirmed in this investigation reached 1.4 billion won. One major case involved unfair gains exceeding 5 billion won, while eight cases involved profits between 500 million won and 5 billion won. The total amount of unfair gains across all referred or notified cases reached at least the 40 billion won range.

Notably, an average of eight virtual asset tokens were used per case. This indicates that price manipulators employed a repetitive tactic of switching between multiple tokens in the short term to generate profits, rather than manipulating a single token over a long period.

Three Major Price Manipulation Tactics Shaking the Crypto Market

In this announcement, financial authorities cited fencing, racehorse, and giant whale as representative price manipulation schemes in the virtual asset market.

  • Fencing: When an exchange suspends deposits and withdrawals for a specific coin, internal supply on that exchange is cut off. Manipulators exploit this environment where arbitrage is impossible to artificially inflate prices. Once deposits and withdrawals resume, the blocked supply floods the market at once, causing prices to plummet and leaving investors who bought at the peak with heavy losses.
  • Racehorse: Manipulators accumulate multiple tokens in advance and then place large buy orders at a specific moment to push them to the top of gainers and trading volume leaderboards. When retail investors mistake the price rise itself for a buy signal and rush in, the manipulators offload their holdings and exit.
  • Giant Whale: A tactic using massive capital to control the market over the medium to long term. Authorities uncovered schemes where manipulators colluded with coin issuance foundations or mobilized overseas exchanges to freeze token supplies for extended periods to maintain target price levels.

Other uncovered schemes included short-term manipulation using borrowed API keys to place automated orders, as well as issuing meme coins, artificially raising prices by spreading fake positive news on social media (SNS), and selling off all holdings at once.

Four Major Vulnerabilities Present in the Stock Market but Missing in Crypto

The prevalence of price manipulation is driven by structural vulnerabilities inherent to the virtual asset market.

First is the lack of public disclosures. Unlike the stock market, mandatory disclosure rules are inadequate, leaving investors with no basis to verify reasons behind sudden price spikes. Second is thin liquidity, where low-volume tokens suffer massive price swings even with small amounts of capital. Third is the absence of market circuit breakers. Trading continues 24/7/365, with weak safety mechanisms such as price fluctuation limits or trading halts. Fourth is exchanges' control over deposits and withdrawals; when an exchange blocks deposits and withdrawals for a token, it becomes an isolated island separated from external markets, preventing arbitrage.

Phase 2 Digital Asset Legislation and Future Measures

To strengthen surveillance against unfair trading of virtual assets, financial authorities are considering two systems under the second phase of digital asset legislation: a suspension of payments system to immediately freeze funds in illegal accounts under investigation, and an insider reporting and reward system tailored to the nature of price manipulation where identifying collusion is critical.

Authorities aim to elevate the crypto market monitoring system to the level of the existing stock market. Experts advise investors to remain cautious of the risks of investing in rapidly surging tokens with unclear drivers or tokens with restricted deposits and withdrawals until institutional safeguards are fully established.
