Anti-Immigrant Protests Intensify in South Africa over Job Losses, Spurring Regional Conflict

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 5 min read

Source: KBS 세계는지금 · 남아공 ‘흑흑 갈등’ 심화, 이민자 대탈출 (KBS_460회_2026.07.11.방송). Summarised from that video.

Large-scale anti-immigration protests and acts of violence and looting targeting immigrants are raging across South Africa. Protesters claim that undocumented foreigners are taking away jobs and commercial rights from South Africans, pressing them to leave the country voluntarily.

Immigrants Suffer Heavy Losses Amid Protesters' Violence and Looting

Anti-immigration groups and protesters wearing traditional Zulu attire and carrying weapons took to the streets, attacking immigrant shops and residences. As the protests intensified, consecutive incidents of looting, arson, and shootings occurred, leaving an estimated minimum of around 10 foreigners dead, and police have arrested over 900 protesters.

Princess Adjei, an immigrant from Ghana who suffered from the attack, expressed her anxiety, saying that all items in her shop were looted, leaving her homeless.

"여기서 립스틱이나 화장 도구 같은 많은 것을 팔았어요. 그런데 보다시피 아무것도 없습니다. 전부 가져갔습니다."

"I used to sell a lot of stuff here, lipsticks, makeup stuff, and all those things. But apparently there's nothing here. Everything is gone."

Immigrants Scapegoated Amid Economic Despair

While the blame for unemployment, poverty, and crime experienced by many South Africans is being directed at immigrants, statistical indicators show that the actual cause lies in structural economic hardship. According to the International Monetary Fund (IMF), South Africa's unemployment rate in 2024 stands at approximately 34%, the second highest in the world, with job losses in mining and manufacturing as well as low growth cited as the primary causes.

Conversely, according to United Nations (UN) data, the number and proportion of international immigrants in South Africa actually decreased from 3.16 million (5.6%) in 2015 to 2.63 million (4.1%) in 2024.

Political analyst Zolani Dube analyzed that dissatisfaction over extreme economic inequality between Black and white populations, which has persisted even after the abolition of apartheid, is being directed at African immigrants, who are relatively vulnerable.

"남아공에는 무슬림도 있고, 인도계도 있으며 백인도 있습니다. 하지만 이곳에서 표적이 되는 건 힘이 없고 접근하기 쉬운 사람들, 바로 아프리카 출신 이민자들입니다."

"We have Muslims, we have Indians, we have Caucasians. But the targets here are those who are powerless and are those who are accessible to us, of which are the African immigrants."

Diplomatic Tensions Escalate with Neighboring African Nations Amid Limited Government Response

South African President Cyril Ramaphosa warned against the protesters' violent acts while admitting to shortcomings in enforcing controls on illegal immigration.

"불법 이민에 대한 엄격하고 집중적인 단속이 부족했던 부분이 있습니다. 그 점은 인정합니다."

"There have been weaknesses in rigorous and focused enforcement. We admit that."

Anti-immigration groups and illegal vigilante groups are pressing for the forced detention and voluntary departure of immigrants, vowing to continue weekly protests until the local elections. In response, governments of neighboring countries including Nigeria, Ghana, and Malawi are dispatching chartered flights and buses to urgently evacuate over 25,000 of their citizens, while diplomatic tensions between nations are also mounting, with the Ghanaian government postponing a scheduled visit by the South African president.
