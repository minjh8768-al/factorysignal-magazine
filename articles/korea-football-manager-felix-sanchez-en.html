Félix Sánchez Emerges as Candidate for National Football Team Manager: His Career and Tactics

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 3 min read

Source: 새벽의 축구 전문가 · "아시안컵 우승경력" 대표팀 임시감독, 펠릭스 바스도 지원했다?! 한국에 어울리는 감독일까. Summarised from that video.

With the submission of applications for the next national football team manager position concluded, Félix Sánchez, who formerly led the Qatar national team, is being mentioned as a candidate. According to Algerian media reports, Manager Sánchez reportedly showed interest in the South Korean national team manager post during negotiations over the manager position with the Algerian Football Federation.

Career from Asian Cup Title to Copa América Quarterfinals

Manager Félix Sánchez coached FC Barcelona's youth team before managing Qatar's youth national teams and senior national team. At the 2019 Asian Cup, he led Qatar to the title by defeating South Korea in the quarterfinals and overcoming Japan in the final.

Although he was dismissed after suffering three consecutive losses in the 2022 Qatar World Cup finals, he subsequently took charge of the Ecuador national team and reached the quarterfinals of the 2024 Copa América. He has continued his career across Asian and South American stages, also winning a league title with Qatari club Al Sadd.

Asymmetric Three-Back System and Pitch-Widening Tactics

While alternating between a back four and a back three, Manager Sánchez frequently deploys an asymmetric three-back structure in practice. He utilizes the pitch widely by pushing athletically gifted full-backs high up the field, drawing the opposing defense to the flanks to secure space for central midfielders.

As expected of a Spanish manager, key characteristics include making wide use of the pitch, emphasizing link-up play through central passing combinations, swift counterattacks, and solid defensive balance.

Evaluation and Outlook as a Temporary Manager Candidate

From the perspective of the national team, which needs to appoint a temporary manager under a short-term system, evaluations suggest that a manager with an understanding of Asian football and experience in short-term tournaments could be a useful option.

However, if the team transitions to a long-term permanent managerial system after the Asian Cup, a broader pool of candidates, including coaches proven on the European stage, may apply. Therefore, the evaluation of Manager Sánchez is expected to be made cautiously between a long-term perspective and short-term achievements.
