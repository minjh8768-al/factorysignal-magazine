Crypto Tax Set for 2027 Enforcement Sparks Controversy Over 'No Loss Carryforward' Rule

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 4 min read

Source: 크립토화이트 · 2027년 가상자산 과세, XRP BTC에 미치는 영향은? 완벽정리 (코인세금폭탄 '이것' 준비하세요). Summarised from that video.

Taxation on cryptocurrency investment gains is scheduled to come into full effect starting January 1, 2027. Through a tax reform plan, the government has virtually finalized its policy to classify gains from cryptocurrency investments as other income and levy a 22% tax.

Controversy Over KRW 2.5 Million Basic Deduction and Lack of Loss Carryforwards

Under the cryptocurrency tax plan, a basic deduction will apply to only up to KRW 2.5 million of profits generated over one year. On amounts exceeding this, a total tax of 22%—combining a 20% national tax and a 2% local tax—will be levied, with the first tax return to be filed during the global income tax reporting period in May 2028.

The biggest controversy among investors stems from the impossibility of loss carryforward deductions. For instance, if an investor incurs a loss of KRW 100 million in 2027 and makes a profit of KRW 50 million in 2028, the account remains in a deficit of KRW 50 million over the two years. However, the tax authorities will not recognize the previous year's loss and will levy a 22% tax of KRW 10.45 million on KRW 47.5 million—calculated by subtracting the KRW 2.5 million basic deduction from the KRW 50 million profit in 2028.

Broad Scope of Taxation Extending to Coin-to-Coin Swaps and Staking

The scope of taxation is also set to be very broad. Not only selling coins for Korean won withdrawals, but coin-to-coin exchanges (swaps), wallet transfers, and paying for goods or meals with coins are all regarded as asset dispositions and included under taxable events.

Coins received through airdrops and exchange staking interest income are also classified as general income and taxed. Furthermore, if the purchase unit price cannot be clearly substantiated, the first-in, first-out (FIFO) accounting method will be applied, calculating tax under the assumption that the coins bought at the lowest price in the past were disposed of first. In this case, even if an investor barely broke even by averaging down during a prolonged bear market, on paper it could be treated as a large gain, resulting in a heavy tax burden.

2027 OECD CARF Launch and Trading Volume Cliff

Claims by some that tracking by the National Tax Service can be avoided by using overseas exchanges or private wallets are untrue. Starting in 2027, the Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework (CARF) led by the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) will go live in major countries. Even when using overseas exchanges, relevant transaction details will be automatically reported to South Korea's National Tax Service.

Ahead of the implementation of the tax plan, liquidity in the domestic cryptocurrency market is shrinking rapidly. Total trading volume across the five major domestic exchanges fell by 54.6% compared to the immediately preceding period, and cumulative trading volume in July declined further by 16.9% compared to the previous month.

Overseas Precedents and Challenges Ahead

Major foreign nations are tending to adjust their systems after going through tax policy missteps. India suffered a 70% plunge in trading volume and capital flight after introducing a 30% tax, while Japan, which taxed up to 55%, is moving toward a 20% separate tax starting in 2025. In contrast, Germany fully exempts capital gains tax on cryptocurrency held for more than a year, supporting digital asset holding.

Advice is emerging that because the tax plan is unavoidable, investors must prepare countermeasures themselves. To prevent disadvantages arising from the National Tax Service's uniform application of the FIFO method, investors should record transfer histories between domestic and overseas exchanges, wallet movement records, and accurate purchase unit prices in detail in Excel or notepad files in advance to secure explanatory documentation.
