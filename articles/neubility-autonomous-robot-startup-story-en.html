How Neubility CEO Lee Sang-min Overcame a 2-Month Runway to Raise 26 Billion Won

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 5 min read

Source: EO Korea · 대학생 창업으로 550억 투자 유치한 연세대 출신 창업가 | 뉴빌리티 이상민. Summarised from that video.

Lee Sang-min, CEO of Neubility, a company that develops camera-based physical AI autonomous delivery and patrol robots, shared the fierce story of his startup journey and crisis recovery, growing from a rocket club into a business operating around 400 robots.

A Dream Born from Watching the Naro Launch Leads to First Place in NASA Competition

CEO Lee Sang-min began dreaming of the aerospace field in middle school upon hearing the news of the successful Naro rocket launch. After entering high school, he formed and participated in the Korea Youth Aerospace Society, and later took on a project aimed at solving space station toilet breakdown issues.

우주 변기 하나 만드는 데 부품이 한 4만 개 정도 들어간다. 원심분리기 원리에서 힌트를 얻어 나선형 구조와 펌프 시스템으로 문제를 해결했다.

"It takes about 40,000 parts to make just one space toilet. Taking a hint from the principle of a centrifuge, we solved the problem using a spiral structure and a pump system."

With a space toilet solution completed through self-study of fluid mechanics, thermodynamics, and 3D design, the Neubility team dramatically won first place in a NASA-related international competition, gaining the opportunity to communicate with astronauts on the International Space Station.

Rocket Development Funding Shortage and the Start of Machine Learning-Based 'Neubility'

After entering university, CEO Lee Sang-min and his team members formed a rocket production club. As rocket manufacturing costs increased exponentially, they funded their work through various part-time jobs, including tutoring, construction work, and game development and sales. Facing their limits, they applied to a startup support program, secured 50 million won in funding, and incorporated Neubility in October 2017.

Combining 'Neuro' (meaning neural network) and 'Ability' (meaning possibility), Neubility built its technical capabilities by carrying out outsourced project work based on machine learning and image processing technologies that could generate immediate revenue instead of rocket manufacturing.

Why Choosing 'Autonomous Robots' Instead of Automobiles or Drones

Securing image-processing-based positioning (location measurement) technology through outsourced projects, Neubility evaluated three sectors to apply this technology: automobiles, drones, and robots. Unlike the automotive market dominated by major finished vehicle corporations or the drone market monopolized by Chinese companies, they noted that the robot market lacked dominant enterprise giants with market capitalizations over 1 trillion won.

During actual robot production, they faced numerous unexpected hardware and software issues, such as straight-line driving errors caused by suspension play and camera electromagnetic interference (EMI). CEO Lee and his team members worked on factory floors and studied self-taught day and night to overcome technical limitations one by one.

Overcoming Regulatory Barriers and the Birth of a Market Brought by Legal Revisions

When encountering regulatory hurdles in commercializing autonomous robots, Neubility chose a detour strategy by first targeting the security patrol robot market on private property where legal restrictions were lighter. Furthermore, through regulatory sandboxes, they secured large-scale robot operation data and demonstrated the necessity and effectiveness of robots.

Following 2020, as non-face-to-face service demand surged due to the COVID-19 pandemic and labor shortages worsened, public perception of robots shifted. Subsequently, amendments to the Road Traffic Act granted certified autonomous driving robots status equivalent to pedestrians, opening the path for full-fledged robot commercialization.

The 2-Month Runway Crisis and Raising Series B Investment

In late 2024, while pursuing a 35 billion won Series B investment round, sudden shifts in circumstances and shrinking investor sentiment caused promised investments to be canceled in succession, reducing the company's runway to two months in its greatest crisis.

CEO Lee Sang-min endured by mobilizing every available measure to pay salaries, including staff reductions, loans, and asking client companies for early execution of contract payments. Ultimately, he secured commitments from investors including Envisioning Partners, successfully closing a Series B investment totaling 26 billion won.

어떤 상황이 있더라도 포기만 안 하면 그 속에 길이 있고 교훈이 있다. 스타트업은 문제를 계속 해결해 나가는 마라톤과 같다.

"No matter what the situation is, as long as you do not give up, there is a way and a lesson inside it. A startup is like a marathon where you continuously solve problems."

CEO Lee Sang-min emphasized that the core of startup survival is faithfully executing today's plan rather than being swayed by grandiose long-term plans, and taking an attitude where founders and teams take center stage to directly solve problems on the ground.
