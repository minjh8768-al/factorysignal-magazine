U.S.-Venezuela Oil Deal Sparks Controversy Over Transparency and Feasibility

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 3 min read

Source: KBS 세계는지금 · 베네수엘라 석유 빅딜, 초대박인가 공수표인가 (KBS_467회_2026.09.12.방송). Summarised from that video.

A mega oil deal has been signed with the United States in Venezuela, the country with the world's largest crude oil reserves, amid recurring large-scale power outages and economic difficulty. U.S. President Donald Trump announced that the largest oil trade deal in world history had been concluded, but controversy is growing over the deal's effectiveness and transparency.

U.S. Claims "100-Year Energy Dominance"... Development of 17 Oil Fields in Venezuela

U.S. President Trump announced via social media that the U.S. had signed a large-scale oil agreement with Venezuela. Under the deal, the U.S. will develop 17 oil fields in Venezuela and secure control and right of first refusal over 65 billion barrels of crude oil, which accounts for more than 20% of Venezuela's total oil reserves.

The White House and U.S. government officials assessed that the deal would serve as an opportunity to secure energy dominance for the next 100 years without burdening American taxpayers. U.S. Energy Secretary Chris Wright stated that the agreement would bring enormous benefits to the people of both countries.

We are highly confident the way the deal is structured and the plans going forward will bring enormous benefits to the people of Venezuela and to the people of the United States.

"We are highly confident the way the deal is structured and the plans going forward will bring enormous benefits to the people of Venezuela and to the people of the United States."

Venezuelan Interim Government Sees Deal as Opportunity to Rebuild Industry

Delcy Rodríguez, interim president of Venezuela, emphasized that the agreement could yield ripple effects such as increased oil production, job creation, infrastructure investment, and higher government revenues. Some local residents, including merchants, also expressed optimism that oil industry facilities would be restored and the economy revived.

However, experts including former employees of Venezuela's state-run oil company, PDVSA, pointed out that recovering the oil industry requires a massive investment of at least $40 billion to $50 billion, but Venezuela lacks such funds. Analysis also suggests that it will be difficult to carry out the announced development projects with aging facilities that have been neglected and idle for decades.

Opposition and Residents Question "Opaque Deal... Doubtful Real Benefits"

Venezuelan opposition leader María Corina Machado strongly criticized the lack of transparency in the deal-making process. Machado emphasized that her country's oil resources belong to the Venezuelan people, not an illegitimate regime.

Aún no se conoce el verdadero alcance de este acuerdo. ¿Quién firma? ¿Qué es lo que realmente se firma? El patrimonio de nuestro suelo no le pertenece a un régimen ilegítimo, le pertenece al pueblo venezolano.

"We still do not know the true scope of this agreement. Who signs? What is really being signed? The heritage of our soil does not belong to an illegitimate regime; it belongs to the Venezuelan people."

Among local residents suffering from daily power outages lasting several hours, skepticism is growing that the agreement will not provide tangible help to people's lives, alongside increasing calls for a change of regime.
