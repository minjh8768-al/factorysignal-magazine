ECB Raises Rates Further in Preemptive Move Against Energy Supply Shocks

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 5 min read

Source: 경제 읽어주는 남자(김광석TV) · [속보] ECB 올해 두번째 금리 인상…"중동전쟁 물가 압박 계속" [즉시분석]. Summarised from that video.

The European Central Bank (ECB) has decided to raise eurozone benchmark interest rates further. As the bank was initially considering a freeze while continuing a rate-cutting stance, upward pressure on inflation driven by changing international conditions and oil price fluctuations appears to have marked a turning point in monetary policy decisions.

Eurozone Rate Hike and Monetary Policy Trends in Major Economies

The eurozone benchmark interest rate had fallen to the 2.15% level through a series of rate cuts, but settled at the 2.65% level after implementing a rate hike to respond to inflationary pressures. As monetary policy moves among major global economies diverge, the eurozone, South Korea, and Japan joined the ranks of rate-hikers, while countries such as the United States and the United Kingdom maintained their existing interest rates, responding in line with their respective economic conditions.

Differences from the 2022 Hyperinflation Phase

This rate hike is distinctly different in nature from the major tightening cycle in 2022 immediately following the pandemic. In 2022, eurozone consumer price inflation recorded hyperinflation exceeding 10%, whereas the recent inflationary trend remains relatively low, with peak levels in the 3% range.

Differences are also apparent in the structural factors driving inflation. In the past, all-around factors such as rapid demand growth, fiscal policy, and supply chain disruptions played a role, whereas energy supply shocks dominate the recent phase. Spillover effects on prices in other sectors and demand-side pressures are evaluated as limited.

Rebound in International Oil Prices and Preemptive Action

As international oil prices show signs of rebounding again, they have emerged as a threat to price stability. Central banks are interpreted as having preemptively raised rates to prevent rising oil prices from further stimulating inflation.

However, as the eurozone's annual economic growth forecast continues at a low level, the likelihood of it leading to sustained inflation driven by broad-based demand growth remains low.

Impact on the Foreign Exchange Market and Outlook

The relative strength of the euro and the yen could exert downward pressure on the dollar. Such exchange rate fluctuations directly and indirectly affect U.S. export conditions and the converted value of investments in the United States.

In summary, the eurozone's recent rate hike is interpreted not as a transition to a major high-interest-rate stance like in the past, but as a limited measure to preemptively respond to oil and energy volatility.
