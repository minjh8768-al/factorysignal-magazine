Breaking Into the US Market with 'Ad-Block Recovery': How Ad-Shield CEO Yu Ju-won Hit 15 Billion Won Net Revenue with 15 Staff

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 5 min read

Source: EO Korea · 2026년 미국에서 가장 빨리 크고 있는 스타트업의 성공 비결 | 애드쉴드 유주원. Summarised from that video.

Yu Ju-won (유주원), co-CEO of Ad-Shield—a company that developed a solution to recover ad revenue lost by websites to ad blockers—shared his story of overcoming startup failure, breaking into the US market, and his philosophy on running a lean, high-performing team.

Choosing the Field Over College in High School

CEO Yu entered Korea Digital Media High School after coming across hacking by chance as a middle school student. Motivated by his talented classmates, he chose to jump straight into the field instead of going to college. Starting from his freshman year in high school, he worked as a freelance app developer for startups, commuting between his school in Ansan and an office in Seoul while living in a 300,000 won-a-month goshiwon in Seocho-dong.

Yu stated that because he chose a path different from others, he was determined to prove himself through results.

The Failure of the First Startup and the Moment of Pivot

In 2019, Yu developed an app to bypass internet censorship that gathered 1 million users in just a few months, and later launched an ad-blocking service that blocked YouTube ads at users' request. Although it recorded 200 million won in revenue in its first month, the business faced limits to expansion when platform terms-of-service revisions created a risk of account restrictions.

With only a few months of runway left, Shin Won-ho (신원호), CEO of early investor Base Ventures, advised letting go of attachment and pivoting. Shin advised that if the CEO's passion and the core team—the essential elements of the business—remained solid, it was right to accept failure and take on a new challenge.

CEO Shin Won-ho explained the situation at the time as follows.

회사가 망하는 이유는 딱 하나밖에 없거든요. 대표님이 포기하면 그만하는 거예요. 근데 그전까지는 사실 전혀 절대 끝나지 않는 게임이거든요.

"There is really only one reason why a company fails. It stops when the CEO gives up. But until then, it is actually a game that never ends."

Tackling the US Market with 'Reverse Thinking'

Yu looked at the ad-blocking market from the opposite angle to find a new opportunity. Instead of ad blocking from the user's perspective, he conceived 'Ad-Shield', a reverse-thinking solution that recovers ad revenue for media publishers who suffer annual losses of up to 300 trillion won due to ad blockers.

Feeling the limitations of the Korean market, he decided to enter the US in 2024. To compensate for his insufficient English skills and sales capabilities, he brought on co-founder Dustin Cha as co-CEO and expanded his network by attending local conferences and events in the US.

Gaining the trust of media publishers based on a product performance 3 to 10 times higher than competitors, Ad-Shield began establishing its foothold in the global market.

15 Billion Won in Net Revenue Built with Talent Density and AI

After initial struggles, Ad-Shield restructured around a core team of 5 to 6 people and reorganized into an easily integrable product. Achieving rapid growth thereafter, Yu realized that adding headcounts to an organization does not automatically lead to increased speed.

To maintain speed, Yu established a hiring philosophy that prioritizes 'talent density' above all else. Adhering to the principle of not hiring anyone who is not deemed superior to existing team members, he introduced AI automation and workflows across operations to generate explosive productivity with a small team.

As a result, Ad-Shield achieved 15 billion won in net revenue in 2025 with a headcount of 15, attaining a performance equivalent to recovering over 4 billion won in annual revenue per employee. Yu stated regarding his future goals:

저희 다음 목표는 이제 30명으로 연이익 500억, 기업 가치는 약 1조 정도 될 거라고 예상을 하고 있습니다. 30명으로 충분히 가능하다고 생각을 하고 있고...

"Our next goal is an annual profit of 50 billion won with 30 people, and we expect a company valuation of about 1 trillion won. I think it is fully possible with 30 people..."

Yu went on to deliver an encouraging message that if Korea's outstanding talents fiercely challenge the global market, they stand a strong chance of success.
