From Data Analysis to Humanoids: The Spreading AI Boom and Its Looming Shadows

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 5 min read

Source: KBS 세계는지금 · AI가 바꿀 미래, 그 빛과 그림자 (KBS_2026.07.25.방송). Summarised from that video.

As artificial intelligence (AI) technology rapidly spreads across society, including battlefields and factory floors, it is boosting efficiency, but concerns continue to be raised that it is simultaneously causing serious side effects such as job losses and environmental pollution.

Artificial Intelligence Penetrates Battlefields and Psychological Warfare

Artificial intelligence technology was actively utilized alongside large-scale cyberattacks during joint operations between the United States and Israel. Military authorities introduced AI to analyze satellite, drone, aerial, and maritime data to identify enemy military facilities and calculate strike routes.

Brad Cooper, commander of the U.S. Central Command, emphasized the role of AI systems during an operational briefing.

These systems help us sift through vast amounts of data in seconds, so our leaders can cut through the noise and make smarter decisions.

"By utilizing AI systems, vast amounts of data can be analyzed in seconds to make decisions faster than the enemy."

U.S. military authorities adopted Anthropic's AI model Claude, using it to strike more than 1,000 targets within 24 hours of launching the operation. Meanwhile, as sophisticated AI deepfake videos circulate, an aspect of "narrative warfare" is emerging, with footage such as sinking warships or harmed children being exploited in psychological warfare.

Humanoid Robots Deployed En Masse to Industrial Sites

In places such as Fujian Province, China, humanoid robots equipped with high-resolution cameras and precise control functions have been deployed to harvest tea leaves without damaging them. The hand capabilities of humanoids are evolving from simple grasping to precise control and tactile-based stages.

Humanoid robot technology has evolved through a first generation focused on simple motor functions and a second generation centered on repetitive tasks to a third generation that uses AI to perform tasks autonomously. Chinese automakers began deploying humanoids to electric vehicle factory floors starting in 2024, significantly increasing operating hours through features such as autonomous battery replacement.

Tech Industry Layoff Wave and 'Jobpocalypse' Concerns

Advances in AI technology are leading to fears of job loss. Major U.S. tech companies are carrying out large-scale layoffs, citing AI adoption and restructuring. In the first half of 2026 alone, more than 150,000 people lost their jobs in the tech industry, and AI was recently named the top reason for layoffs.

Jensen Huang, chief executive officer (CEO) of Nvidia, expressed a critical view regarding companies using AI as an excuse for layoffs.

AI became productive and useful only six months ago. There were somehow laying people off two years ago because of AI. It doesn't make any sense. It was just a way for them to sound smart, and I really hate that.

"AI only became a truly productive and useful tool six months ago. Yet companies were laying people off two years ago citing AI. It makes no sense. It was just an excuse for them to sound smart, and I really hate that attitude."

In a 10-hour parcel sorting match between humans and humanoids, humans won by a narrow margin, but humanoid robots demonstrated the performance to stably process more than 240,000 items over 200 hours without rest. Automaker GM also introduced collaborative robots en masse in places where workforce was reduced. According to public opinion polls, negative perceptions of AI were found to be high among young Americans aged 18 to 34.

Depletion of Water Resources Caused by Surge in Data Centers

In regions with a high concentration of data centers, such as the U.S. state of Texas, issues of environmental pollution and water depletion have surfaced. To cool data center servers, a large data center consumes between 1,100 metric tons and up to 10,000 metric tons of water per day.

As a result, water levels in nearby lakes and groundwater are dropping rapidly, but only 17% of data center operating companies disclose their water usage. Because there are no statewide Texas regulations mandating the disclosure of future water usage plans, concerns among local residents persist.
