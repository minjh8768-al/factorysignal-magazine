Big Consulting Firms That Outearned OpenAI Face Crisis as 'AI Agents' Shake Up Market

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 5 min read

Source: 티타임즈TV · AI에 일자리를 위협받는 경영 컨설턴트. Summarised from that video.

Over the past two to three years since the generative AI boom began, the companies making the most profit were not technology developers, but major consulting firms such as McKinsey, Boston Consulting Group (BCG), and Accenture. This was due to a flood of advisory requests from companies struggling with how to integrate generative AI into their businesses. Recently, however, as clients build their own AI agents and tech companies enter the consulting market directly, the standing of major consulting firms is shaking significantly.

Major Consulting Firms Were the Biggest Beneficiaries of the AI Boom

Since generative AI emerged as a major topic, key consulting firms have enjoyed an unprecedented boom. While tech developers like OpenAI and Google shouldered massive development costs, consulting firms reaped real profits by providing AI adoption roadmaps and frameworks.

In the case of Accenture, generative AI-related contract bookings stood at around $300 million in fiscal year 2023, but related revenue jumped tenfold to $3 billion in 2024. In 2025, contract volume is expected to reach $5.9 billion (approximately 8.455 trillion won). In contrast, while OpenAI recorded $3.7 billion in annual revenue in 2024, its operating loss reached $5 billion, prompting assessments that consulting firms took home the actual profits.

Boston Consulting Group (BCG) also recorded all-time high revenue of $13.5 billion in 2024, with 20% of total revenue generated from AI advisory work. McKinsey & Company similarly conducted about 400 generative AI projects in the first half of 2024 alone, with roughly 40% of its overall work focused on AI and technology consulting.

Adoption of Internal AI Agents and Large-Scale Restructuring

Consulting firms also rapidly adopted generative AI internally to improve operational efficiency. McKinsey developed an in-house AI chatbot named 'Lilli,' trained on over 100,000 internal knowledge assets, enabling more than 75% of all employees to use it in their work. The introduction of Lilli reduced employees' work time by about 30%, and a total of 12,000 AI agents—including tone adjustment and industry-specific agents—were applied across overall operations.

BCG provided 'ChatGPT Enterprise' to all 33,000 employees, building more than 18,000 customized GPTs. It also introduced 'Deckster,' a presentation editor trained on over 900 slide templates, significantly cutting writing time for staff. Deloitte built 'Sidekick' and 'Zora AI,' while PwC constructed 'Agent OS' to manage more than 250 internal agents.

However, productivity gains through AI adoption led to headcount reductions. McKinsey reduced its workforce from over 45,000 in 2023 to about 40,000 as of 2025, a cut of more than 10%. Accenture also conducted large-scale restructuring targeting personnel who could not keep up with AI technology, laying off more than 11,000 employees over three months.

'Smarter Clients' and the Onslaught of AI Consulting Startups

The real crisis for consulting firms began with changes among clients. Using AI agents' 'deep research' capabilities, clients can now directly perform in 10 minutes analysis work that used to take teams of junior consultants weeks to complete. As a result, questions are being raised about the necessity of multi-million-dollar consulting contracts, and some clients are demanding fee reductions based on efficiency gains.

At the same time, small boutiques and startups breaking the traditional 'pyramid structure' (a few seniors + many juniors) of major consulting firms are increasing. Unity Advisory, founded by former partners, built teams without juniors using only senior consultants and proprietary AI tools. SaaS-based AI consulting startups are also encroaching on the market, such as Dialog, which provides AI-driven market research solutions, and Xavier AI, which bills itself as "McKinsey in a Box" and generates strategy presentation materials in minutes.

OpenAI's Direct Entry and an Era of Tech Convergence

Direct market entry by technology companies is also pressuring major consulting firms. OpenAI has made a full-scale entry into its own consulting services for large enterprises and government agencies, building customized AI models and applications using proprietary corporate data. A prime example is its $200 million contract with the U.S. Department of Defense.

Experts predict that in a situation where the boundary between technology and consulting is blurring, traditional consulting firms will see their position narrow further if they fail to prove human-only creative insights beyond simple data collection and analysis.
