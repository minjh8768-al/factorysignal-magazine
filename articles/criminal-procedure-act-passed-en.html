Democratic Party-Led Criminal Procedure Act Amendment Passes; Political Factions Call It 'Trophy' and 'Boomerang'

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 8 min read

Source: SBS 뉴스 · [정치사냥꾼] '보완수사권 전면 폐지' 국회 본회의 통과..'공소기각' 요건 확대 / SBS / 뉴스헌터스. Summarised from that video.

The National Assembly, led by the Democratic Party, passed an amendment to the Criminal Procedure Act, abolishing the prosecution's direct investigative powers for the first time in 70 years. As a result, the prosecution's supplementary investigative powers have completely disappeared, and the grounds for dismissal of prosecution by the court have also been expanded, leading to various interpretations and predictions within and outside political circles. This issue, which arose during President Lee Jae-myung's overseas trip, along with the controversy over a constitutional amendment for re-election and the deterioration of economic public sentiment, are expected to serve as a test of leadership after his return next week.

Amendment to Criminal Procedure Act: Complete Abolition of Prosecutorial Investigative Powers

On July 31, the National Assembly passed the amendment to the Criminal Procedure Act at a plenary session with 175 votes in favor, 2 against, and 1 abstention out of 178 members present. Accordingly, the prosecution's supplementary investigative powers were completely abolished, and the prosecutor's direct investigative powers disappeared into history after 70 years.

Political commentator Lee Seok-hyun assessed that the ruling party had nothing to smile about regarding the passage of this amendment. He pointed out that it was passed amid internal party conflict and that there were many negative results in public and party sentiment polls. Lee analyzed that hardliners within the Democratic Party inevitably sought a 'trophy' related to prosecutorial reform ahead of the party convention, and the government also responded to this in order to reduce variables. He added that the Democratic Party 'endured a beating and passed it'.

Jeong Kwang-jae, director of the Dongyeon Political Research Institute, described the passage of this amendment as 'truly absurd and a cause for bitter laughter'. He expressed concern that the abolition of the prosecution's direct investigative powers in the criminal justice system after 70 years marks the beginning of 'an age of Armageddon'. Director Jeong warned and criticized that criminals would slip through the widened net and the suffering of crime victims would accumulate.

정광재 동연정치연구소장은 “범죄자들은 이렇게 넓어진 그물 사이를 쑹쑹 통해 다니고 범죄 피해자들의 피해는 쌓일 수밖에 없는 그런 시대가 앞으로 올 것 같아서 정말 걱정입니다”라고 우려했다。

"I am truly worried that an era is coming where criminals will freely move through such a widened net and the suffering of crime victims will inevitably accumulate."

Controversy over Expanded Grounds for Dismissal of Prosecution and Political Interpretations

The amendment to the Criminal Procedure Act also includes provisions expanding the grounds on which courts can dismiss a prosecution, sparking controversy. The opposition party is claiming that this provision is intended to prevent President Lee Jae-myung's case from going to trial.

Director Jeong Kwang-jae criticized the Democratic Party for explaining it with 'political logic' and mentioned that even Lawyers for a Democratic Society (Minbyun) does not agree with the complete abolition of supplementary investigative powers. He stated that not only the general public but also the legal community is concerned about whether 'a proper criminal justice system can operate like this', and assessed that the safeguards prepared by the Democratic Party make complete supplementation difficult. Director Jeong questioned why two conditions were added when there were already six grounds for dismissal of prosecution, claiming that President Lee Jae-myung would be the first to utilize this widened door.

정광재 동연정치연구소장은 “공소 기각과 관련해서는 이미 여섯 개의 공소 기각 요건이 있어요. 그걸로도 충분히 될 수 있는데 왜 그러면 두 개를 추가했느냐? 일단 공소 기각의 문을 넓혀 놓는 겁니다”라고 지적했다。

"Regarding the dismissal of prosecution, there are already six grounds for dismissal. That would be sufficient, so why add two more? It is to first widen the door for dismissal of prosecution."

Commentator Lee Seok-hyun refuted that viewing this provision as a 'deal' between hardliners and the presidential side is 'speculation'. He explained that the controversy over the dismissal of prosecution is primarily a matter of truth, and secondarily an area of political judgment. While seeing that its practical significance is not great because prosecution can be reinstituted even after dismissal, Lee said that it is difficult to be free from criticism that 'it gave an excuse' by deliberately adding this content during a politically sensitive period.

Piled-up National Tasks and a Test of Leadership

In addition to the passage of the amendment to the Criminal Procedure Act, President Lee Jae-myung's administration currently faces several adverse factors, including stock market instability, deteriorating public sentiment regarding real estate, and controversy over a constitutional amendment for presidential re-election. In particular, all these controversies erupted during President Lee Jae-myung's overseas trip, and are expected to act as a significant burden on state affairs after his return.

Jeong Hee-yong, Secretary-General of the People Power Party, urged President Lee Jae-myung to directly 'request reconsideration (veto)' on this amendment if he considers the livelihoods and people. However, Director Jeong Kwang-jae predicted that if President Lee Jae-myung exercises a veto before the Democratic Party's national convention on August 17, a situation could arise where the candidate he supports does not become party leader. Therefore, he would make a 'choice faithful to political interests', and the possibility of exercising a veto is close to 0%.

Director Jeong Kwang-jae argued that attention should be paid to the fact that Rep. Kwak Sang-eon, son-in-law of former President Roh Moo-hyun, was the only Democratic Party lawmaker who cast a dissenting vote regarding this amendment to the Criminal Procedure Act. He stated that the Roh Moo-hyun spirit means 'being able to make decisions that even supporters oppose if it is for the national interest', and assessed that only Rep. Kwak Sang-eon properly embodied the Roh Moo-hyun spirit, while the remaining Democratic Party lawmakers 'betrayed the Roh Moo-hyun spirit'.

Regarding economic issues, commentator Lee Seok-hyun expressed concern that real estate issues or stock market instability could be a 'fatal blow', and especially that tax hike issues would have a greater impact. He predicted that while the recent tendency of Democratic Party convention candidates to emphasize housing supply and express negative views on taxation shows an attempt to respond sensitively to public sentiment, a major debate would unfold within the Democratic Party, as taxation requires National Assembly approval.

Conclusion

The controversy surrounding the passage of this amendment to the Criminal Procedure Act shows a complex intertwining of political calculations, livelihood issues, and concerns about the foundation of the rule of law. President Lee Jae-myung's administration is expected to face a test of leadership on how it will resolve these domestic and international difficulties after his return.
