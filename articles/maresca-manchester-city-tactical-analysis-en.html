Enzo Maresca’s Manchester City: Tactical Changes and Build-Up Challenges

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 4 min read

Source: Football Made Simple · This Is Life After Pep (Tactical Analysis). Summarised from that video.

Manchester City played their first match under manager Enzo Maresca, who took charge following the resignation of Pep Guardiola. Manchester City's tactical structure shown in the pre-season friendly against Inter provided clear hints as to how Maresca will lead the team in the future.

Changes in Building Up from the Back and Goalkeeper Usage

The core of Maresca's approach lies in building up from the back. Early in the match, considering the characteristics of goalkeeper Gianluigi Donnarumma, Manchester City actively utilized long passes toward the frontline striker when under opponent pressure.

In short goal-kick situations, they attempted to form a deep diamond with Mateo Kovacic as a single pivot and the center-backs spreading wide. In the first half, they struggled against Inter's blocking pressure, but in the second half, Donnarumma delivered direct passes to the pivot even under pressure, establishing an automated pattern that allowed unmarked center-backs to advance and raising possession from 53% in the first half to 76% in the second half.

Asymmetric 3-2-5 Formation and Emphasis on 'Verticality'

When developing attacks, Manchester City created a 3-2-5 (3-BOX-3) formation through asymmetric inverting, with Rico Lewis moving from right-back into midfield and the left-back tucking in as a back-three stopper.

Unlike the slow, gradual positional attacks of the past, the concept most emphasized in this match was 'verticality.' The front three attackers stayed on the shoulder of the opponent's defensive line to continuously exploit space behind, while Phil Foden and Tijjani Reijnders took on the role of pushing the defensive line back from the central half-spaces. This effectively widened the space between Inter's defensive line and midfield line.

4-2-4 Defensive Block and Problems with Pressing Timing

When defending, Foden pushed forward to form a high 4-2-4 defensive block. The wingers tucked inside to block passing lanes to the center, and the two central midfielders man-marked the opponent's number 8 midfielders.

However, flaws were revealed in the completeness of the defensive structure and the pressing coordination among players. If the pressing timing was even slightly late, a single line-breaking pass from Inter neutralized both the midfield and back four lines simultaneously, or dangerous situations occurred where center-backs were dragged wide to mark the opponent's wide wing-backs.

Future Outlook and Challenges

Despite maintaining high possession throughout the match, Manchester City recorded only two big chances over the 90 minutes. As Inter tightly blocked the center, penetrating through the middle became difficult, and the team ultimately relied heavily on the 1-on-1 individual capabilities of wingers such as Semenyo and Savinho.

As it was the first pre-season match, the precision of pressing and the detail of build-up play remain tasks to be improved through future training. How manager Maresca harmonizes the tactical legacy he displayed at Leicester City and Chelsea with Manchester City's existing principles appears to be the key to success or failure in the regular season.
