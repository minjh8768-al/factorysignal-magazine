Why Exchange Rate Surged Despite Strong Exports: Capital Flows and FX Conversions Outweigh Interest Rate Gaps

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 4 min read

Source: 경제 읽어주는 남자(김광석TV) · 환율 왜 이렇게 흔들릴까? 진짜 원인은 ‘금리’가 아니었습니다 | 경읽남과 토론합시다 | 백석현 연구위원 [1편]. Summarised from that video.

An analysis indicates that capital movements and supply and demand conditions in the foreign exchange market are the primary drivers behind the sharp fluctuations in the won-dollar exchange rate, despite strong exports and a trade surplus trend.

Foreign Selling Outweighing Trade Surplus

Baek Seok-hyun (백석현), a senior research fellow at Shinhan Bank, analyzed recent won-dollar exchange rate trends and noted that it has become difficult to explain exchange rate movements solely through current account or trade surpluses. He explained that while dollar inflows increased significantly due to strong semiconductor exports, foreign stock selling and capital outflows exceeded these inflows, driving the exchange rate upward.

Senior Research Fellow Baek pointed out that the scale of selling by foreign investors was larger than the volume of dollars supplied to the market by exporting companies.

수출로 달러가 엄청나게 들어온 건 맞는데, 그 수출 업체들이 달러를 계속 시장에 풀었음에도 불구하고 외국인들이 더 많이 팔고 나간 거죠.

"It is true that a huge amount of dollars came in through exports, but even though those exporters kept releasing dollars into the market, foreigners sold and left in even greater numbers."

Delays in Corporate Currency Exchange and Foreign Exchange Authority Intervention

Delays by exporting companies in converting dollars into won amid expectations of a rising exchange rate were also cited as a factor exacerbating the supply-demand imbalance. This was driven by the perception that delaying the conversion of dollars into won is advantageous for companies as the exchange rate rises.

Senior Research Fellow Baek explained that after the upward trend in the exchange rate steepened, a combination of factors—including the authorities' strong determination to stabilize the market, decisive responses, and concentrated dollar selling by major corporations—helped turn the exchange rate downward.

기업들 입장에서는 아무래도 달러 가진 입장에서는 환율이 오를수록 팔 때 이득이니까 지연해서 팔고 이런 문제가 없지는 않았습니다.

"From the perspective of companies holding dollars, because it is more profitable to sell as the exchange rate rises, there were indeed issues such as delaying their sales."

Limitations of Single Variables Such as Interest Rate Gaps and Money Supply

Pointed out as well were the limitations of predicting exchange rates based solely on single indicators, such as the key interest rate differential between South Korea and the United States or the M2 money supply growth rate. Senior Research Fellow Baek emphasized that data from the past 10 years shows extended periods where the Korea-U.S. interest rate gap and the won-dollar exchange rate moved in opposite directions, noting that the exchange rate is determined by a combination of more than 1,000 diverse variables, including the stock market, prices, and the currencies of major nations.

Professor Kim Kwang-seok (김광석) also summarized that while interest rate hikes or money supply adjustments can contribute to exchange rate stability to some extent, liquidity in the foreign exchange market and economic agents' confidence in future direction are the key elements driving changes in supply and demand.
