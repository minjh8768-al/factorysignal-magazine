CEO Kim Seojoon: "In the AI Era, We Should Consider Distributing Means of Production Instead of Tax Redistribution"

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 5 min read

Source: 티타임즈TV · “어떤 신이 될 것인가는 내 선택에 달렸다” (김서준 해시드 대표). Summarised from that video.

As artificial intelligence (AI) technology becomes an essential life infrastructure like electricity or telecommunications, overall social and economic structure changes are accelerating. Outlooks suggest that traditional formulas based on academic background or capitalist profit maximization are breaking down, while productivity gaps based on AI access will widen.

Collapse of Traditional Success Formulas and AI Infrastructure

In the past, class rankings, College Scholastic Ability Test (CSAT) scores, or making a lot of money served as clear metrics of success, but analysis indicates these criteria could become meaningless in the AI era. As AI becomes a basic means of survival like electricity or water, the need for a national-level response is also being raised.

This is the background behind arguments that, just as the state provides compulsory education to citizens, it should distribute AI tokens that grant access to AI computing resources free of charge. This is because the productivity difference between those who can use AI and those who cannot could widen by hundreds of times.

Limits of Robot Tax and the Distribution of Means of Production

Limitations are pointed out regarding wealth redistribution methods through ex-post tax collection, such as the previously discussed robot tax or AI tax. This is because it is realistically impossible to measure the efficiency and impact of various software agents individually to establish taxation criteria.

Accordingly, rather than collecting remaining profits as taxes after economic activity and redistributing them, an alternative approach is proposed: pre-emptively sharing equity in or access to key means of production, such as AI data centers and models.

AI 시대의 핵심 생산수단은 컴퓨팅과 AI 모델 접근권이다. AI가 부가가치를 창출하는 순간부터 생산수단 자체를 배분하는 모델이 지속 가능한 사회를 만들 수 있다.

"The key means of production in the AI era are computing power and access to AI models. A model that distributes the means of production themselves from the moment AI creates added value can build a sustainable society."

Opportunities for Korean Startups in the Japanese Market

The Japanese market is seeing a surge in demand for B2B AI transformation (AX) amid population decline and labor shortages. While internal initiatives move slowly due to a conservative and cautious corporate culture, interest in Korean companies equipped with fast execution and high technology adoption is growing.

Recently, as Korean character chat platforms, medical, and beauty services achieve results in the Japanese market, Japanese venture capital firms are actively considering investments in Korean startups. The need to combine complementary infrastructure and technology from both countries to form a single large economic zone is also being discussed.

AI Solving Complex Problems and the Role of Humans

Recently, AI has expanded its horizons beyond finding answers to questions posed by humans, solving problems related to the Navier-Stokes equations—one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems in mathematics—on its own, reaching domains not yet defined by humans.

In an era when technical boundaries continue to expand, experts suggest that rather than clinging to short-term plans, society should consider a sustainable future by collaborating with entrepreneurs who solve unresolved societal problems through capital and technology.
