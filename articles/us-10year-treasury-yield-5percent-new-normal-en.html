US 10-Year Treasury Yield at 5% Range Is New Normal... 'Basis for Fed Rate Hike Still Valid'

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 4 min read

Source: 매일경제TV · [이슈&피플] 국채금리·물가·고용지표, 연준 결정에 미칠 영향은? / 장재철 / 피나클 경제연구소 대표. Summarised from that video.

As the US 10-year Treasury yield surged to the 5% range, reaching its highest level since 2007, an analysis suggested that this high-interest-rate environment has established itself as a new normal.

Jang Jae-chul, head of Pinnacle Economic Research Institute, appeared on a broadcast to analyze the causes of the recent spike in US Treasury yields and project its impact on the Federal Reserve's monetary policy and the domestic stock market.

Upward Pressure on Yields Driven by Real Rates and Treasury Supply Surge

Jang stated that the rise in the 10-year US Treasury yield to the 5.2% range can be evaluated as having settled in as a new normal.

5% 이상의 10년물 금리는 계속 지속되지 않을까 이렇게 전망해 볼 수가 있을 것 같습니다.

"I think we can project that 10-year yields above 5% will likely persist."

Jang pointed out that the biggest feature of this yield increase was that real interest rates rose instead of inflation expectations. He explained that out of the 5.2% 10-year Treasury yield, the rise in real interest rates accounted for 2.8 to 2.9 percentage points, which is the highest level in 18 years since 2008. In addition, he cited increased supply volume as a major factor pushing up long-term yields, as the US government issues over $1.8 trillion in Treasuries annually due to its large fiscal deficit.

Narrowing Yield Curve Inversion... "Signal of Tightening, Not Recession"

Regarding the narrowing yield gap between US 10-year and 2-year Treasuries, he interpreted it as a tightening effect of monetary policy rather than a signal of an economic recession.

Jang analyzed that unlike typical yield curve inversions caused by declining long-term yields, the current situation is one where short-term rates are rising due to the Fed's benchmark rate hikes while long-term rates are also rising due to fiscal deficits and supply burdens, narrowing the gap. He added that since the US economic growth rate forecast for the third quarter reaches around 3.5%, rather than entering an immediate recession, it is necessary to observe whether consumption and investment slow down due to prolonged high interest rates.

Strong Inflation and Employment Data... Basis for Fed Rate Hikes Maintained

He pointed out that even if high market rates are formed, the necessity for the Fed to raise benchmark rates does not completely disappear. Although market tightening is underway with mortgage rates reaching the 7% range, he noted that there is a view within the Fed that rising market rates cannot replace policy rate hikes.

In addition, regarding the outlook for the upcoming August Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) price index and September employment report, he assessed that both inflation and employment provide justification for rate hikes. Core PCE inflation in August is expected to reach 3.4% year-on-year and headline PCE 3.7%, exceeding the Fed's target of 2%, while the labor market remains solid with the unemployment rate holding around 4.1% and the number of employed people increasing.

Sensitive Reaction of Domestic Stock Market... "Due to Global Capital Flows and Market Structure"

He cited the structural characteristics of the Korean market as the reason why the domestic stock market reacts more sensitively to rising US Treasury yields than the US stock market.

When interest rates rise, global fund managers respond to increased capital borrowing costs and higher discount rates on future earnings by first selling stocks in emerging markets that are easy to cash out and have recently risen significantly. Jang explained that because representative semiconductor stocks such as Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix account for a very high proportion of over 50% in the KOSPI market, the domestic stock market is particularly heavily affected by foreign selling pressure.
