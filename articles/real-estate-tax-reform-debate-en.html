Government Announces Real Estate Tax Reform: Debates Brew Over Tax Normalisation vs. Housing Supply Priorities

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 3 min read

Source: SBS 뉴스 · 세제 개편안 놓고도 성한용-허민 충돌‥정치부 대기자들의 엇갈린 분석 / 성민종합정치 / SBS / 주영진뉴스브리핑. Summarised from that video.

As the government released a real estate tax reform proposal targeting high-priced homes and non-resident properties, debates over tax fairness and the impact on the housing market are heating up.

Normalisation of the Tax System and Raising Issues of Fairness

Government officials emphasized the need to normalize the tax system, pointing out that the tax burden on real estate capital gains has been significantly lower compared to earned income tax.

국민 여러분께서도 세금 얘기하면 참 정부 입장에서도 어렵습니다. 인기 있는 일도 아니고요. 그러나 조세 제도가 이렇게 정상화되지 않으면 형평성 문제가 생깁니다.

"When talking about taxes with the public, it is truly difficult from the government's standpoint as well. It is not a popular thing to do. However, if the tax system is not normalized in this way, issues of fairness will arise."

Government officials stated their position to overhaul the existing system—which grants excessive tax benefits to non-resident single-home owners or high-priced home owners solely for holding property long-term—to focus instead on actual residents.

Priority on Supply Measures and Market Concerns

Former reporter Heo Min, who appeared on a broadcast, expressed partial agreement with the intent of the tax reform while voicing regret over the setting of priorities.

Heo raised concerns that the lock-in effect of property listings could intensify in major areas such as Gangnam, with the burden potentially being passed on to tenants paying monthly or lump-sum rents. Consequently, he pointed out that preparing housing supply measures and stabilizing the stock market are more urgent tasks than tax reform.

Bipartisan Cooperation and Need for National Assembly Debate

Senior reporter Seong Han-yong, who also appeared on the show, interpreted this tax reform not merely as a tool to curb housing prices, but as a process of normalising the tax system itself.

While viewing temporary confusion and backlash as inevitable when tax systems change, Seong stressed that the ruling and opposition parties must engage in deep discussions in National Assembly standing committees to create consistent laws and policies that the public can predict.

Future Outlook and Challenges

As the real estate tax reform proposal moves forward in a direction that reduces benefits for ultra-high-priced property owners and non-residents, whether protective measures for actual residents and effective supply plans can be implemented in parallel is expected to be a key issue in future political developments.
