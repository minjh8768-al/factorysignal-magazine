US Employment Data Shows Surprise Strength... Fed More Likely to Freeze Rates Than Hike

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 4 min read

Source: 경제 읽어주는 남자(김광석TV) · [속보] 고용 서프라이즈로 국채금리가 급등했다가, '트럼프의 강한 금리요구 발언'으로 국채금리 진정 [즉시분석]. Summarised from that video.

Recent employment data released by the US Department of Labor significantly exceeded market expectations, reconfirming the solid state of the US economy. Kim Kwang-seok (김광석), head of economic research at the Korea Institute for Economics and Industry, evaluated the impact of the strong labor market on inflation and interest rates while analyzing the future direction of Federal Reserve monetary policy.

Unemployment Rate at 4.1%... Non-Farm Payrolls Far Exceed Expectations

According to the published employment figures, the US unemployment rate stood at 4.1%, meeting market expectations and maintaining a stable level of employment. In particular, non-farm payrolls increased by 161,000, far surpassing the market expectation of 56,000.

Average hourly earnings growth also reached 3.1% year-on-year, topping the market forecast of 3.0%. Director Kim commented that all three key employment indicators—unemployment rate, job growth, and wage growth—showed strong results, confirming that the US labor market and economy remain solid.

Rate Hike Caution and Treasury Yield Surge Driven by Strong Jobs Data

Kim explained the connection among employment, inflation, and interest rates, citing the Phillips curve and monetary policy mechanisms. If the labor market is solid and the economy is strong, consumer demand and spending are sustained, which could slow the pace of disinflation and provide justification for the Fed to keep interest rates high or consider additional hikes.

Immediately following the release of the employment data, market caution mounted regarding the possibility of Fed rate hikes and prolonged high interest rates. Consequently, the US 10-year Treasury yield surged, and financial market volatility also broadened.

Fed Officials' Remarks and the Possibility of Disinflation

Remarks by key Fed officials support this context. New York Fed President Williams noted that the surge in Treasury yields was a result of a strong US economy. Conversely, Fed Governor Christopher Waller assessed that some signals of disinflation were appearing.

"Surge in Treasury yields is the result of a strong economy"

Kim analyzed that if the upcoming Consumer Price Index (CPI) and Producer Price Index (PPI) continue their downward trends, expectations of inflation peaking out will gain traction.

Monetary Policy Outlook and Likelihood of Rate Freeze

Regarding future benchmark rate decisions, Kim noted that because the Fed's current policy rate remains in real positive territory higher than the inflation rate, there is little justification for further rate hikes unless inflation rebounds sharply.

However, he projected that early rate cuts would also be difficult given that core inflation has not yet fully reached the target level. Taking external variables such as Middle East tensions and upcoming inflation indicators into comprehensive consideration, Kim suggested that the Fed is most likely to hold rates steady for the time being.
