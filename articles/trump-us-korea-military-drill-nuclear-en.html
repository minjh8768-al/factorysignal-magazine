Trump’s Order to Downsize US-ROK Drills and '57 Nukes' Remark Driven by Political Calculus: Expert

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 5 min read

Source: KBS 1라디오 · [정관용의 시사본부] 지금 세계는 | 11월 트럼프-김정은 만나나? | 조한범 통일연구원 석좌연구위원 | KBS 260823. Summarised from that video.

Attention is focusing on the background and repercussions of former US President Trump's instructions to scale back joint US-ROK military exercises and his specific mention of the number of North Korean nuclear weapons. Experts analyze this as a move driven by US domestic political interests and aimed at resuming future negotiations with North Korea, pointing out that the South Korean government needs to devise meticulous alternative measures.

Background and Impact of the Order to Downsize Joint US-ROK Drills

In a broadcast interview, Cho Han-bum, a senior research fellow at the Korea Institute for National Unification (KINU), assessed former President Trump's instruction to downsize joint exercises as a measure driven by political calculus rather than military necessity. He analyzed that since stopping joint drills was North Korea's strongest demand during past inter-Korean and US-North Korea summits, it could be a signal to draw North Korea back to the negotiating table.

Senior Research Fellow Cho noted that it was unusual for reductions or changes to take place on the day of the exercise, adding that US Forces Korea and US military authorities likely felt considerable embarrassment. In particular, he pointed out that because this exercise was linked to the Full Operational Capability (FOC) verification—a key step for the transfer of wartime operational control (OPCON)—the downsizing of the drill could also disrupt the schedule for verifying OPCON transfer.

을지연습은 우리 정부의 대규모 재난 및 전쟁 대비 훈련이며, 한미 상호방위를 위한 연합훈련은 지속적으로 실시되어 왔다. 그러나 정치적 결정에 따라 훈련 축소가 이뤄진 것은 이례적인 일이다.

"The Ulchi exercise is our government's large-scale drill in preparation for disasters and war, and joint exercises for US-ROK mutual defense have been conducted continuously. However, it is an unusual occurrence for drills to be scaled back due to political decisions."

Meaning Behind the Specific Mention of '57' North Korean Nukes

Analysis also continued regarding former President Trump explicitly citing "57" as the number of North Korea's nuclear weapons. Senior Research Fellow Cho explained that unless North Korea discloses it itself, it is difficult to determine the exact number of nuclear weapons, noting that experts and international organizations usually provide estimated ranges around 60, or between 80 and 120.

He analyzed that presenting a specific number is highly likely his characteristic way of speaking to show off that he has detailed intelligence on North Korea. He added that the US officially using expressions like "nuclear weapons" or "nuclear capability" instead of the term "Nuclear Weapon State" is a strategy to maintain ambiguity—acknowledging the practical threat while avoiding the burden of official recognition under international law.

Concerns Over Policy Shift from Denuclearization to Nuclear Arms Control

Regarding observations that Washington political circles are leaning toward nuclear freeze or arms control negotiations instead of complete denuclearization, Senior Research Fellow Cho diagnosed this as "forced realism." This means that with North Korea continuously advancing its nuclear capabilities, freeze negotiations to first halt further development could emerge as a realistic alternative.

However, while this could be a way for the US to manage threats to its own homeland, it poses a dilemma for South Korea by effectively tolerating North Korea's existing nuclear arsenal. Consequently, the South Korean government faces the task of securing practical deterrence while officially maintaining the goal of complete denuclearization.

Tasks for South Korea to Secure Independent Deterrence

As alternatives South Korea must secure in response to the North Korean nuclear threat, Senior Research Fellow Cho cited advancing the Korean Three-Axis System along with acquiring nuclear-powered submarines and securing rights for uranium enrichment and reprocessing. He emphasized that South Korea should build independent deterrence capabilities by referring to Japan's past case of securing enrichment and reprocessing rights in the form of prior consent through the US-Japan Nuclear Cooperation Agreement.

Regarding the possibility of resuming a future US-North Korea summit, he projected a high likelihood that North Korea would respond to dialogue, given that it also knows it has no economic future without sanctions relief from the US. It is a crucial moment for the South Korean government to exercise strategic bargaining power to protect national interests amid this volatile situation.
