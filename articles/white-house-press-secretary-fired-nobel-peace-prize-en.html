White House Sacks Shortest-Serving Press Secretary as Trump Rails Against Nobel Peace Prize Decision

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 3 min read

Source: KBS 세계는지금 · 최단기 대변인 / 평화의 대통령 (KBS_470회_2026.10.17.방송). Summarised from that video.

Shortest-Serving White House Press Secretary Dismissed After 5 Days

New White House Press Secretary Katie Zachariah was abruptly dismissed just five days after taking office. Kim Jae-cheon, a professor at the Graduate School of International Studies at Sogang University, explained that Zachariah earned the ignominy of becoming the shortest-serving press secretary in White House history.

Former Press Secretary Zachariah appeared nervous during her first briefing, ending it in just 14 minutes after taking questions from only three reporters. Subsequently, a credential controversy erupted when it was revealed that she had previously been introduced as a lawyer during past broadcast appearances despite not holding a law license.

As the controversy spread, former Press Secretary Zachariah clarified that while she had failed the California bar exam, she had never posed as an attorney and was merely providing legal analysis. However, members of the U.S. legal community pointed out that using the title of lawyer with only a law degree is inappropriate, and providing legal analysis could also pose ethical issues.

Trump's Impulsive Appointments and Inadequate Vetting Highlighted

Ahn Byong-jin, a professor at the Global Academy for Future Civilizations at Kyung Hee University, analyzed that President Trump has a tendency to impulsively appoint individuals he likes while watching broadcast television. Critics point out that this incident occurred because the complex U.S. personnel vetting process was bypassed.

Furthermore, Professor Ahn evaluated this as a glaring example of a trend in the Trump administration to apply strict standards and easily fire female officials who put him in difficult situations.

Nobel Peace Prize Winner and Trump's Pushback

Navi Pillay, a former judge at the International Criminal Court (ICC), was selected as this year's Nobel Peace Prize recipient. In response, President Trump expressed dissatisfaction at missing out on the award, claiming he had ended eight wars during his presidency.

The Nobel Prize was given out today to a liberal person who works at the United Nations who nobody has ever heard of. We should have had eight Nobel Prizes for each war.

"The Nobel Prize was given out today to a liberal person who works at the United Nations who nobody has ever heard of. We should have had eight Nobel Prizes for each war."

ICC Sanctions and the Controversy Over Upholding International Law

The Trump administration previously signed an executive order imposing sanctions on ICC officials regarding the authorization of investigations into U.S. military war crimes and Gaza-related matters. Professor Kim Jae-cheon explained that these sanctions differ from previous ones in that they directly targeted the ICC as an institution itself.

Citing Navi Pillay's remarks, Professor Ahn Byong-jin emphasized that no power, no matter how strong, can be exempt from judgment under international law and legal norms.
