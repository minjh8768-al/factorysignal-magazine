Worst Flu Outbreak in 10 Years... Vaccination Urged Amid Spread of Variant Virus

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 5 min read

Source: SBS 뉴스 · 독감, 코로나19까지 '대유행'.."서둘러야해!" 심상치 않은 상황 / SBS 뉴스속보. Summarised from that video.

As flu cases rise rapidly, concerns are being raised that this could become the worst flu season in 10 years. According to statistics from the Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency, the number of suspected flu cases per 1,000 outpatients has surged, showing a significant increase compared to the same period last year.

The spread of the flu is particularly steep among school-aged children and adolescents. The number of suspected flu cases was highest among children aged 7 to 12, with cases also concentrated among teenagers aged 13 to 18.

Cases Surge Despite High Vaccination Rate... Variant Virus to Blame

Behind the sharp increase in patients, despite higher vaccination rates among high-risk groups and children this year compared to last year, is a mutation of the flu virus. Subclade J, a sub-lineage of the H3N2 virus, was used to manufacture this year's flu vaccine, but the virus frequently detected in South Korea recently has been confirmed to be a mutated form, Subclade K.

When discrepancies occur between the vaccine strain and the circulating strain, the effectiveness of preventing infection may be somewhat reduced. However, an official from the Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency emphasized the importance of vaccination.

백신주가 현재 유행하고 있는 바이러스와 유사해서 독감을 예방하는 데 충분히 효과가 있습니다.

"The vaccine strain is similar to the currently circulating virus, so it is sufficiently effective in preventing the flu."

Concerns Over Simultaneous COVID-19 Wave... "Timely Vaccination Recommended"

Along with the flu epidemic, hospitalized COVID-19 patients are also steadily increasing, raising growing concerns about a twindemic in which both infectious diseases circulate simultaneously. Experts emphasized that if symptoms such as high fever, coughing, or difficulty breathing persist, individuals should visit a medical institution early for treatment.

Because it takes about two weeks for antibodies to form after vaccination, the Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency urged people to complete timely vaccinations in preparation for a full-scale outbreak and to follow personal hygiene guidelines such as handwashing and wearing masks.
