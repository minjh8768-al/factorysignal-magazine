North Korea's Missile Launch and the 'Joseon' Naming Controversy: Assessing Korean Peninsula Security

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 5 min read

Source: KBS 1라디오 · [정관용의 시사본부] 지금 세계는 | 정동영 "북한을 조선으로 부르는 것 제안" | 조한범 통일연구원 석좌연구위원 | KBS 260809. Summarised from that video.

Cho Han-bum (조한범), a senior research fellow at the Korea Institute for National Unification, analyzed the security situation surrounding the Korean Peninsula, including North Korea's recent short-range ballistic missile launch intentions, the controversy over changing North Korea's name raised by some within the Ministry of Unification, and the redeployment of US air defense assets.

Single Launch and Absence of Media Reports... Background Behind North Korea's Missile Provocation

North Korea recently launched a suspected short-range ballistic missile for the first time in 42 days. It is evaluated as unusual that only a single missile was fired and that North Korean media released no notable reports following the launch.

Cho Han-bum, a senior research fellow at the Korea Institute for National Unification, mentioned the possibility that the missile belonged to the KN-23 or KN-24 series mainly used on the Ukrainian front lines, and presented several possibilities regarding the background of the launch.

북한이 러시아로 보내기 전 사전 성능 검증을 위해 시험 발사를 한 것인지, 아니면 발사 과정에서 중간에 소실되어 실패한 것인지 등 여러 가능성이 존재합니다.

"There are several possibilities, such as whether North Korea conducted a test launch to verify performance before sending it to Russia, or whether it failed due to being lost midway during launch."

Dr. Cho also explained that the possibility of a new weapon system test conducted during an on-site guidance session cannot be ruled out, given that State Affairs Commission Chairman Kim Jong-un's villa is located in Wonsan, the launch site.

'North Korea' or 'Joseon'... Proposal to Change the Name and Constitutional Issues

Controversy surrounding the name continues as it was recently reported that the Minister of Unification had proposed using the name 'Joseon' instead of 'Bukhan' (North Korea).

Dr. Cho pointed out the complex reality arising from the difference in perspective between North Korea's status under international law and the Constitution of the Republic of Korea.

국제법적으로 북한은 별개의 국가이지만 헌법상으로는 우리의 미수복 영토입니다. 정식 국호를 부르는 것은 대한민국의 정통성을 인정하는 맥락과 충돌할 수 있습니다.

"Under international law, North Korea is a separate state, but under our Constitution, it is our unrecovered territory. Calling it by its official state name could conflict with the context of recognizing the legitimacy of the Republic of Korea."

Dr. Cho analyzed that while inter-Korean agreements state the official title 'Democratic People's Republic of Korea', completely switching to it as the official designation risks clashing with public sentiment. Consequently, he offered the opinion that maintaining strategic ambiguity between ideal and reality is the desirable response at present.

Redeployment of US Air Defense Assets and Its Impact on Korean Peninsula Security

Analyses have emerged suggesting that as the United States faces a missile inventory shortage due to instability in the Middle East and other regions, part of the air defense assets—including Patriot and THAAD systems deployed with US Forces Korea and across Asia—have been moved to the Middle East.

Dr. Cho analyzed this as an example demonstrating that US military assets move according to global strategy rather than solely for US domestic security.

사드 탄약 재고의 70~80%, 패트리엇의 절반가량이 차출되었다는 분석이 나올 만큼 미국도 급작스러운 전력 소모에 직면해 있습니다.

"The United States is also facing sudden asset depletion, to the point where analyses suggest 70–80% of THAAD ammunition inventory and about half of Patriot systems have been drawn down."

However, he cautioned against excessive concern, stating that the South Korean military is establishing its own medium- and high-altitude air defense network, such as the Cheongung-II, and that this will not lead to an immediate security vacuum.

Japan's Establishment of a National Intelligence Organization and Moves Toward Becoming a 'Normal State'

Meanwhile, Japan officially launched a National Intelligence Council and a National Intelligence Bureau for the first time since World War II to unify its intelligence gathering and analysis functions.

Dr. Cho evaluated this step as part of Japan's evolution into a normal state capable of waging war, in tandem with securing counterstrike capabilities.

통합 정보 기관 출범과 토마호크 미사일 도입 등은 일본이 기존의 안보 틀에서 벗어나 전력 강화를 가속화하고 있음을 보여줍니다.

"The launch of an integrated intelligence agency and the introduction of Tomahawk missiles demonstrate that Japan is breaking out of its existing security framework and accelerating its military buildup."

Dr. Cho emphasized that amid growing global uncertainty, South Korea must closely monitor changes in Japan's security policy and formulate strategies to respond.
