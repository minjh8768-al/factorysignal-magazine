US Launches Record 'Operation Suffocate Iran' Sanctions, Sending Ripples Globally

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 4 min read

Source: KBS 세계는지금 · 이란 질식 작전 (KBS_465회_2026.08.29.방송). Summarised from that video.

US Declares 'Operation Suffocate Iran', Expanding Sanctions Significantly to Third Parties

The United States has declared its strongest economic sanctions in history against Iran. Moving beyond existing sanctions centered on crude oil, finance, and manufacturing, five additional sectors used by Iran as bypass routes—including cryptocurrency, advanced technology, and shipping—have been added to the sanctions list.

Park Hyun-do (박현도), a professor of religious studies at Sogang University, explained that the operation name 'Economic Suffocation Operation' reflects the US determination to cut off Iran's funding lines by strictly applying secondary boycotts, which punish third-country companies or individuals engaging in transactions with Iran under collective responsibility.

Domestic Pressures in the US and Background Behind Shift to Economic Strategy

Behind the shift in US strategy from military pressure to economic sanctions lie domestic economic and political instability, as well as the burden of long-term military operations, including a sharp drop in missile inventories.

Kim Je-cheon (김제천), a professor at the Graduate School of International Studies at Sogang University, analyzed that as inflation fueled growing dissatisfaction among farmers—with ground beef prices rising 12.5% year-on-year and diesel prices soaring 53%—the need grew for the US administration to seek an exit from the war and demonstrate results.

Iranian Economic Crisis Deepens: Skyrocketing Prices and Fuel Shortages

Due to strong US blockade measures, the internal economic crisis in Iran is deteriorating to the worst conditions. The annual inflation rate has surpassed 80% for three consecutive months, and prices of food and daily necessities surged 130% in a single month.

Furthermore, as fuel imports are blocked due to the naval blockade, Iranian authorities are experiencing severe fuel shortages, limiting refueling to 20 liters per session, and protest demonstrations by local residents are being observed once again.

Participation of Major Nations Including China and Future Outlook

Whether the US economic sanctions against Iran succeed depends on the participation of China, Iran's largest trading partner, but China has made its opposition to US sanctions clear. As the largest trading partner importing more than 80% of Iran's oil, China has refused to join unilateral US sanctions, citing its Anti-Foreign Sanctions Law announced in 2021.

In addition, neighboring countries such as Pakistan and Jordan are taking subtle stances, such as calling for a solution through dialogue or seeking summit meetings with China, drawing attention to the future of international sanction coordination against Iran and the trajectory of the US-Iran conflict.
