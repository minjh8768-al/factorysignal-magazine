'Digital Dollars' Devour Blockchain… Why Discussions on Won Stablecoins Are Gaining Momentum

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 4 min read

Source: 지식부장관 · 두나무가 원화 스테이블코인을 노리는 이유. Summarised from that video.

The Dollar's Blockchain Takeover and Concerns Over 'Digital Dollarization'

When Bitcoin first appeared, it drew attention as an alternative currency that could weaken the influence of central banks and the US dollar. However, over time, the exact opposite phenomenon has unfolded in the blockchain ecosystem. Dollar-pegged stablecoins such as USDT and USDC have taken currency dominance, establishing themselves as the most powerful currencies on the blockchain.

According to data from the Bank for International Settlements (BIS), approximately 98% of the stablecoin market value consists of dollar-denominated assets. Dollar-pegged coins have thus solidified their position as the primary medium of exchange circulating in the virtual asset market and blockchain networks. While holding foreign currency previously required currency exchange procedures through banks or securities firms, an environment has now been created where anyone can easily hold and transfer digital dollars with a single smartphone wallet.

Impact on the Domestic Foreign Exchange Market and the Bank of Korea's Warning

The problem lies in what happens if the tokenization of real-world assets and financial assets—such as stocks, bonds, and real estate—proceeds in earnest in the future. If dollar stablecoins are fully adopted as a means of payment in the process of invigorating on-chain financial transactions, even domestic users could get locked into a structure of selling Korean won to buy dollar-pegged coins. This would lead to a decline in demand for won assets, placing a strain on exchange rates and overall monetary policy.

Indeed, Bank of Korea Governor Rhee Chang-yong warned in remarks to the National Assembly about the impact that the spread of dollar stablecoins could have on the domestic foreign exchange market and the value of the won.

달러 스테이블코인에 대한 수요가 거래 구조에 따라 실제 외환시장으로 전달되고 자국 통화의 약세 압력으로 이어질 수 있다고 보고 있습니다.

"We view that demand for dollar stablecoins could be transmitted to the actual foreign exchange market depending on the transaction structure and lead to downward pressure on the local currency."

The Need for a Won Stablecoin and the Competition for Infrastructure Dominance

Under these circumstances, the reason discussions on introducing a won stablecoin continue steadily is to secure a won-based payment system within the domestic digital financial ecosystem, rather than unconditionally blocking the spread of dollar stablecoins. Even with well-established existing banking networks and simple payment services, if tokenized assets are traded on the blockchain without a won coin for settlement, a disconnection from the won financial network is inevitable.

Accordingly, the importance of building a reliable issuance and distribution infrastructure—including issuing entities, regulatory frameworks, and proof of reserve—is growing. Dunamu, which operates the domestic virtual asset exchange Upbit, also recently unveiled its blockchain network 'GIWA Chain' at a conference, presenting its direction as an infrastructure provider supporting stablecoin issuance and distribution. The struggle for dominance surrounding the stablecoin ecosystem is expected to intensify into a race to pre-empt the gateway to future digital financial networks, extending beyond the simple issuance of a single coin.
