US Senate Fails to Pass Clarity Act as SEC and CFTC Pivot Toward Direct Crypto Regulation

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 4 min read

Source: CNBC Television · U.S. regulators step up to regulate crypto after Clarity Act stalls in Senate. Summarised from that video.

Efforts to establish a cryptocurrency regulatory framework in the U.S. Senate have fallen through with the failure of the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act. As congressional legislation hits a standstill, the crypto industry and key regulatory agencies are seeking alternatives by moving to establish independent rules without going through Congress.

Senate Cloture Vote Fails, Derailing Clarity Act

A cloture vote to advance the Clarity Act on the Senate floor ended in 49 votes in favor and 50 against, falling short of the 60 votes required to move the bill forward. The legislation contained comprehensive rules for digital asset tokens, issuer standards, and user protection measures.

Democratic lawmakers strongly opposed the bill, arguing that it was insufficient to prevent high-ranking public officials from profiting through cryptocurrency ventures. Democratic Senator Elizabeth Warren sharply criticized the bill introduced by Republicans.

With this bill, Republicans are not only turning a blind eye to Trump's corruption, they are turbocharging it.

"With this bill, Republicans are not only turning a blind eye to Trump's corruption, they are turbocharging it."

Three Republican senators—Josh Hawley, Jerry Moran, and Susan Collins—also broke from party lines to vote against the measure. Republican Senator Thom Tillis switched his vote to nay in order to preserve the right to submit a motion to reconsider and keep the bill alive.

Crypto Industry Pivots Toward Direct Regulation by SEC and CFTC

With the legislative process in Congress stalled, the cryptocurrency industry is turning its attention toward direct collaboration with executive regulatory agencies. Brian Armstrong, Chief Executive Officer (CEO) of Coinbase, emphasized the need for direct communication with administrative agencies.

At this point, I don't think we can wait on Congress and the Senate. It's in our interest at this point to go work directly with the SEC and the CFTC.

"At this point, I don't think we can wait on Congress and the Senate. It's in our interest at this point to go work directly with the SEC and the CFTC."

Heads of the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) and the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) have expressed that they can establish clear cryptocurrency regulations within their existing authorities. Bernstein analysts projected that following the failure of the Clarity Act, the SEC and CFTC will move swiftly and proactively with rulemaking. Indeed, just two days after the bill's failure, the SEC approved an order clearing a pathway for tokenized stock issuance and the creation of 24-hour trading markets.

State Opposition to Legislation and the Need for Federal Oversight

Tim Scott, Chairman of the Senate Banking Committee, urged the SEC and CFTC to establish clear rules of the road for digital assets until Congress completes legislation. Meanwhile, attorneys general from several states voiced opposition, stating that the bill could infringe upon state authority to regulate securities markets and crack down on fraud.

Aaron Klein, a senior fellow at the Brookings Institution, pointed out that while state governments can actively exercise authority in cracking down on fraud, there are limitations to regulating cross-border capital markets as a whole, making a systematic federal regulatory framework essential.
