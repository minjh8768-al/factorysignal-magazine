Ed Yardeni: Rising Bond Yields Are a Vote of Confidence in Economic Strength

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 4 min read

Source: CNBC Television · The bond market is finally working the way it should work, says Ed Yardeni. Summarised from that video.

Global bond markets are roiled as 30-year government bond yields in Germany and the United States reach multi-year highs. Germany's 30-year treasury yield hit a 15-year high of 3.779%, while the U.S. 30-year treasury yield reached approximately 5.33%, touching its highest level in nearly 20 years.

Bond Vigilante Activity and Government Debt Concerns

Ed Yardeni, president of Yardeni Research who first coined the term "Bond Vigilantes," assessed recent bond market movements by stating that bond vigilantes are acting very actively in countries with high debt-to-GDP ratios, such as Japan, the United Kingdom, and the United States. He explained that bond vigilantes, who were concerned about a fiscal deficit of around $250 billion back in 1983, are now testing the massive U.S. fiscal deficit situation ranging from $1.5 trillion to $2 trillion.

At the end of the day, the bond yield wouldn't be here if the economy wasn't doing well, so I view it as a vote of confidence in the strength of the economy.

"At the end of the day, the bond yield wouldn't be here if the economy wasn't doing well, so I view it as a vote of confidence in the strength of the economy."

Return to 'Normal Levels' Prior to the Financial Crisis

Yardeni diagnosed current 10-year treasury yield levels as a return to the normal 4–5% range seen prior to the global financial crisis. His analysis indicates a departure from the period when the central bank, the Federal Reserve, artificially controlled the bond market by keeping benchmark interest rates near zero, returning to an environment where capital is efficiently allocated according to market principles.

However, the Fed's lack of vigilance regarding inflation, rising oil prices, and geoeconomic risks in the Strait of Hormuz were cited as concerning factors that bond vigilantes continue to watch closely.

Stock Market Optimism Based on Earnings Momentum (FEMO)

Despite concerning factors in the bond market, Yardeni maintained a positive outlook on the stock market. He explained that unlike the "FOMO (Fear of Missing Out)" market of 1999, the current market is being driven by "FEMO (Fabulous Earnings Momentum)" based on solid corporate earnings.

He added that massive capital expenditures by Big Tech companies, post-retirement asset spending by baby boomers holding about $100 trillion in net assets, and increased interest income from money market funds (MMFs) due to higher interest rates are supporting economic resilience. Yardeni Research raised its year-end S&P 500 target from 8,250 to 8,400 without downward revision, while maintaining its long-term forecast of reaching 10,000 points by 2030.
