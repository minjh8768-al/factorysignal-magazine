US ICE Shock Glove Plan Sparks Controversy as Count Binface Surges in UK By-Election

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 4 min read

Source: KBS 세계는지금 · 전기충격 장갑 / 깡통 백작 (KBS_464회_2026.08.22.방송). Summarised from that video.

As controversy continues over excessive enforcement and hardline immigration policies by the U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE), backlash from human rights groups is growing following news of plans to introduce gloves that deliver painful electric shocks. Meanwhile, in the United Kingdom, a candidate representing the satirical character 'Count Binface,' who parodies the political establishment, drew attention by securing over 20% of the vote in a by-election.

U.S. ICE Pursues Electric Shock Gloves, Escalating Crackdown Controversy

Reports have emerged that U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) plans to supply its agents with gloves equipped with low-output electric shock devices (G.L.O.V.E.). While appearing similar to ordinary gloves, pressing a button delivers a powerful electric shock of up to 380V upon contact with the skin. It causes pain intense enough to subdue an adult male in just three seconds.

Originally used primarily in correctional facilities such as prisons and detention centers, this equipment is planned for distribution to field immigration enforcement agents. According to local media, ICE disclosed a plan to purchase up to $20 million worth of equipment by March of next year and distribute it to field agents.

Concerns over potential misuse are also growing due to the nature of the equipment, which relies on the discretion of field agents. A senior reporter in a broadcast interview noted that the decision to use the equipment depends on the judgment of the field agent.

It's really within the discretion of the agent or officer who's wearing them at the time.

"Virtually everything comes down to the discretion of the field agent."

Questions have been raised about the electric shock gloves potentially violating Article 5 of the UN Universal Declaration of Human Rights and the Convention Against Torture. In fact, an incident occurred in 2024 at a detention center in Kentucky, U.S., where a man died after receiving shocks from electric shock gloves and a Taser; an internal investigation confirmed that electric shock glove shocks were administered 27 times, and a lawsuit is underway.

'Count Binface' Wins 26.9% of Vote in UK By-Election, Drawing Eyes to Political Satire Culture

In a by-election for the Clacton constituency in the United Kingdom, satirical character 'Count Binface,' wearing a bin on his head, made an appearance and performed unexpectedly well. Count Binface's real name is Jon Harvey, a British comedian and broadcast writer.

Count Binface has run in a total of seven elections, running in constituencies of former Prime Ministers such as Boris Johnson and Rishi Sunak. While his past vote share was under 1%, he recorded a high vote share of 26.9% in this by-election.

This unusual vote share is analyzed as stemming from major political parties boycotting and putting forward no candidates against party leader Nigel Farage, who re-ran after resigning over unreported political fund allegations, causing opposition votes to rally behind the satirical candidate Count Binface.

U.K. election law permits the use of nicknames and unusual costumes, and is flexible enough that candidates can register with just a £500 deposit and nominations from 10 voters. In the past, in the 2002 Hartlepool mayoral election, a candidate dressed in a monkey mascot suit was elected and successfully went on to serve three terms.

Count Binface was evaluated as satirically highlighting citizens' frustrations through witty pledges such as a price cap on croissants, improved Wi-Fi on trains, and the conscription of public transport speakerphone users.
