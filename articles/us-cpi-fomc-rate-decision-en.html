'Strong Employment, Inflation, and Oil Prices' Emerge as Final Variables for Fed's September Rate Decision

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 3 min read

Source: 이데일리TV · 🚀 [화상연결] 美 금리인상 초읽기?물가 다시 뛰면 ‘9월 인상’ | Market Signal (20260907). Summarised from that video.

Ahead of the Federal Reserve's (Fed) September Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) meeting, inflation indicators and international oil prices are drawing attention as the final variables that will decide the direction of interest rates. With recently released employment data exceeding market expectations and proving the strength of the labor market, whether the Fed will raise interest rates is expected to be determined by upcoming inflation index results.

August PPI and CPI Releases... Market Eyes Focus on Consumer Prices

The indicators that need to be examined most carefully in the US economy this week are inflation figures. The August Producer Price Index (PPI) will be released on the 10th, and the August Consumer Price Index (CPI) will be disclosed to the market on the 11th.

The market forecasts that the August headline CPI will rise 3.4% year-on-year, remaining at the same level as July. Core CPI, excluding food and energy, is expected to slow slightly to 2.4% from 2.5% in July.

Strong Employment Data Expands Possibility of Rate Hike

The impact of this inflation data on the Fed's interest rate decision is greater than ever. As August non-farm payrolls increased by 162,000, significantly exceeding market expectations, speculation that the Fed could raise interest rates to curb inflation has gained traction.

Following the release of the employment report, the probability of a September rate hike reflected in the market rose from the low 50% range to around 60%. Sung Joo-won (성주원), Edaily's New York correspondent, analyzed the possibility of a Fed rate hike as follows:

지난주 고용 지표가 연준의 금리 인상 가능성을 키웠다면, 이번 주 물가 지표는 실제로 금리 인상의 방아쇠를 당길지를 결정할 마지막 핵심 변수다.

"If last week's employment data raised the possibility of a Fed rate hike, this week's inflation figures are the final key variable that will determine whether to actually pull the trigger on a rate increase."

Three Scenarios Depending on CPI Results

The market believes the Fed's policy trajectory will split into three scenarios depending on the CPI release. If the CPI exceeds market expectations, the justification for a September rate hike will be further strengthened. On the other hand, if both headline and core inflation show a clear slowing trend, the possibility of a rate freeze could re-emerge.

If the figures align with market expectations, intense debate within the Fed over whether to raise or freeze rates will be inevitable. As the Fed has entered a blackout period restricting monetary policy-related comments ahead of the FOMC, the market is expected to react more sensitively to the indicators themselves.

US-Iran Conflict and Strait of Hormuz Instability... Oil Price Variable Emerges

Rising international oil prices caused by worsening conditions in the Middle East are also a factor complicating the Fed's calculations. With military clashes recently resuming between the US and Iran, heightening anxiety in the Strait of Hormuz, a key bottleneck for crude oil transportation, international oil prices are facing upward pressure.

If higher oil prices persist, there is a risk that they will spread to transportation costs and overall commodity prices, stimulating inflation expectations. This could lead to concerns over a rebound in US inflation while increasing the burden of rising exchange rates and import prices on the South Korean economy.

Trio of Employment, Inflation, and Oil Prices to Decide Interest Rate Trajectory

Ultimately, the key keywords running through the US economy this week can be summarized as employment, inflation, and oil prices. Amid a strong employment situation, the inflation indicators and oil price trends revealed this week are expected to serve as the final watershed determining the Fed's interest rate decision and the direction of global financial markets.
