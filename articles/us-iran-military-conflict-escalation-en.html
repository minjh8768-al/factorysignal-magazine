US-Iran Military Conflict Escalates, Spreading War Crisis Across Middle East and Strait of Hormuz

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 5 min read

Source: KBS 세계는지금 · 미국 이란 침공 (KBS_2026.08.01.방송). Summarised from that video.

Selection of Third Supreme Leader Amid Leadership Vacuum and Declaration of Retaliation

A major shift in Iran's power structure occurred after a large-scale U.S. military operation killed supreme leader Ali Khamenei and many top military commanders. Iranian media reported that Mojtaba Khamenei, the second son of Khamenei, was selected as the third supreme leader. Newly appointed supreme leader Mojtaba Khamenei vowed revenge for the blood of the martyrs, recommending the closure of U.S. military bases and issuing a strong message of retaliation.

U.S. President Donald Trump expressed his intention to be involved in the selection of Iran's next leader while continuing his hardline rhetoric against Iran.

We are going to hit them extremely hard over the next 2 to 3 weeks. We are going to bring them back to the Stone Ages.

"We are going to hit them extremely hard over the next 2 to 3 weeks. We are going to bring them back to the Stone Ages."

Blockade of Energy Chokepoint Strait of Hormuz and Chaos Across the Middle East

In retaliation, Iran blocked the Strait of Hormuz, a key crude oil transit route, and launched airstrikes against U.S. military bases and key facilities in neighboring countries. Targets included an oil industrial complex in Fujairah, United Arab Emirates, a U.S. military base in Abu Dhabi, and LNG facilities in Qatar, prompting the UAE government to send emergency alerts advising residents to evacuate.

Tit-for-tat airstrikes between Israel and Iran also raged fiercely. Israel launched airstrikes on Hezbollah strongholds in Lebanon, killing a key commander, while Iran directly confronted Israel by firing Sejjil missiles with a range of 2,000 kilometers and cluster bombs at the Israeli mainland.

Negotiations Collapse Amid Grace Periods and Ultimatums

President Trump demanded that Iran fully reopen the Strait of Hormuz and extended the deadline for suspending airstrikes several times. However, U.S.-Iran negotiations held in Islamabad, Pakistan, ultimately collapsed after failing to bridge differences over plans for handling highly enriched uranium. The U.S. demanded a 20-year suspension of uranium enrichment, while Iran presented a restraint proposal capped at five years, leading to a standoff.

Naval Counter-Blockade and a Prolonged Situation

Following the collapse of negotiations, the U.S. deployed warships to the Strait of Hormuz and declared a counter-blockade on vessels using Iranian ports. The U.S. Navy heightened pressure by seizing tankers and cargo ships carrying Iranian crude oil in places like the Indian Ocean. In response, Iran continued its hardline stance by pushing for legislation codifying control over the strait.

Since then, subtle shifts in mood have been detected, such as Iran temporarily allowing merchant ships to pass through the Strait of Hormuz during a truce period and the potential for a second round of peace talks. However, with mutual distrust and military confrontation continuing between the two nations, uncertainty in the Middle East remains unresolved.
