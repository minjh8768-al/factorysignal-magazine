Jordan's Dilemma: Plummeting Tourism and Controversy Over U.S. Troop Presence

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 3 min read

Source: KBS 세계는지금 · 미국 믿었다가 전쟁터 될 판… 요르단의 깊어지는 고민 (KBS_469회_2026.10.10.방송). Summarised from that video.

The ancient city of Petra, carved out of red sandstone cliffs, is a world-renowned tourist destination representing Jordan. However, with escalating military tensions in the Middle East recently, the vibrancy of Petra, once bustling with tourists from around the world, has become hard to find.

Deserted Tourist Sites... Wave of Hotel and Restaurant Closures

According to the local Jordanian tourism industry, the booking cancellation rate for foreign tourists reached 100% last March, 85% in April, and 70% in May. As tourists from Europe and the United States stopped visiting, about 30 out of 70 hotels located in the Petra region suspended operations.

Rosemary, a British tourist, booked a flight to Jordan last March, but had to postpone her trip after her flight was canceled immediately following a ballistic missile attack by Iran on U.S. military facilities in Jordan.

If I hadn't been able to come this time, I would have deferred it to March... A lot of friends kind of were a bit concerned, but I wasn't concerned.

"If I couldn't have come this time either, I would have postponed it again to next March. Many of my friends were worried, but I wasn't."

Even during lunchtime, restaurants and souvenir shops remain empty with no customers. Mahmoud, who operates a restaurant in Petra, expressed his struggles over the burden of rent and maintenance costs.

We pay from our pocket. I pay rent every month 1,200 JOD from my pocket every month. Nobody helps us.

"All expenses come out of my own pocket. I pay about 2.3 million won in rent alone every month. Nobody helps us."

Tourism, Accounting for 14% of GDP, Takes a Hit

In Jordan, which lacks natural resources such as oil and gas, the tourism industry is a key sector accounting for about 14% of gross domestic product (GDP). The decline in tourists is dealing a severe blow not only to hotels and restaurants but also to the regional economy as a whole.

Jordanian economist Ara Khatami analyzed that the size of the tourism industry has decreased by 27% compared to the past, and that flight cancellations and booking withdrawals have clearly had a negative impact on economic activity. Observers note that the downturn in tourism is spreading beyond the accommodation sector to the overall national economy.

Political and Civil Society Backlash Over U.S. Military Presence

As the tourism slump and security concerns deepen, political conflict over the presence of U.S. troops is also surfacing within Jordan. On July 26, in the House of Representatives of the Jordanian Parliament, loud shouts and protests erupted following a lawmaker's proposal to express condolences to U.S. service members killed in an Iranian attack, and the statement was struck from the official record.

On the same day, hundreds of people, including opposition politicians and civil society figures, raised the level of backlash by signing an open letter demanding the withdrawal of foreign troops.

Military Cooperation with the U.S. and Jordan's Dilemma

Jordan has maintained military and security cooperation with the United States for the past 30 years. The United States provides Jordan with at least $1.45 billion (approx. 2 trillion won) in economic and military aid each year, which accounts for about 8% of the entire Jordanian government budget.

Tayseer Abu Ourabi, a member of the Jordanian House of Representatives, emphasized that U.S. support is essential for national development.

ما عندنا اليوم أي دعم أو إحنا ما عندنا موارد اللي إنها بتساعدنا ولكن إحنا المساعدات الأمريكية جزء...

"Today, we do not have any support (other than from the U.S.), nor do we have resources to help us. U.S. aid is part of our national development efforts, and we have no reason to give it up."

Security cooperation with the United States has served as a pillar helping Jordan maintain relative stability and support its tourism industry amidst instability in neighboring countries such as Syria and Iraq. At the same time, however, it has become a factor directly exposing Jordan to regional military conflicts. Maintaining its image as a safe Middle Eastern nation while preserving its relationship with the United States has emerged as the biggest dilemma for the Jordanian government.
