M&A Legend Woo Jun-ho: "Real Business Lies Beyond Numbers"

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 3 min read

Source: 명승은 TV · [인터뷰] 스타트업, 이제 M&A를 고민해야 할 때. (Feat. 우앤파트너스 우준호 대표). Summarised from that video.

Woo Jun-ho, CEO of Woo & Partners and the real-life inspiration behind the drama 'The Art of Negotiation' after successfully executing mergers and acquisitions (M&A) worth hundreds of billions of won, shared his views on the essence of M&A and the attitude startup executives should adopt.

From First Entry-Level Employee on the M&A Team to On-Site Expert

CEO Woo Jun-ho began his career as the first entry-level employee on the M&A team when he joined E-Land Group. Over several years of conducting deals, he felt the limitation that one must understand the essence of business beyond merely buying and selling skills to properly execute M&A.

Accordingly, he voluntarily applied to operational divisions, managing on-site businesses such as hotels to gain practical experience. The true story of CEO Woo returning to Korea at the request of headquarters during an overseas assignment to complete major projects was noted by a writer who was a long-time friend, becoming the motive for a drama storyline.

The Key Secrets Driving Successful M&A

CEO Woo cited "the ability to quickly judge the final picture" as the biggest factor in achieving successful M&A. He said that in the process of reviewing thousands of projects, distinguishing and focusing early on deals that are likely to work and those that will not was the secret to raising the success rate.

He also emphasized the importance of "warm capitalism," saying that M&A and business are ultimately done by people. He explained that the process of integrating members of each organization after a merger or acquisition so that they look in the same direction with one mind forms the foundation of business success.

"Real business lies beyond the numbers. Managing from the very smallest elements and recreating value—that is business."

Advice for Startup CEOs

CEO Woo advised startup executives not to view IPOs (initial public offerings) as the only exit strategy, but to keep M&A in mind as a strategic tool for business expansion and growth.

He urged that rather than becoming consumed by the amount of investment raised, practical management that thoroughly takes care of small business elements, even with small amounts, must take priority.
