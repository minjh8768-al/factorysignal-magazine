NYC Housing Crisis Deepens Despite Rent Freeze as Tenant-Landlord Conflicts Persist

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 5 min read

Source: KBS 세계는지금 · 월세 동결, 뉴욕의 새로운 주거 실험 (KBS_466회_2026.09.05.방송). Summarised from that video.

In New York City, which has some of the highest housing costs in the world, conflicts over soaring rents and housing stability are deepening.

Rent Freeze Policy and the Reality of the Housing Market

Rents in New York City have risen by 30.5% compared to before the pandemic, with Manhattan's median monthly rent reaching around $4,927. As rent increases persisted amid supply shortages, New York City decided to freeze rents with a 0% rate hike for approximately 1 million rent-stabilized apartments.

Charles Jones, a young man from Idaho, is continuing his life in New York through a senior home-sharing program that connects young adults with seniors aged 60 and older to ease the burden of high living costs. Jones pointed out the state of New York's housing market.

The housing market is insane. The cost of living, it's not what it should be for most New Yorkers who can't afford that. You have to make like 40 times the amount you make.

"The housing market is insane. The cost of living, it's not what it should be for most New Yorkers who can't afford that. You have to make like 40 times the amount you make."

Tenant Conditions Neglected Amid Rent Freeze

Although the rent freeze measure was implemented, critics point out that tenant protection remains incomplete. Diana Rodriguez, who lives in a rent-stabilized home in the Bronx, has not received repairs despite her apartment being damaged by a fire upstairs.

Rodriguez said her landlord prefers that tenants leave rather than spending money to repair the home.

Everything is old, but he's not willing to spend the money. He's willing to pay you to leave, but he's not willing to put that money and invest it in the apartment.

"Everything is old, but he's not willing to spend the money. He's willing to pay you to leave, but he's not willing to put that money and invest it in the apartment."

Controversy Over Proposed Wealth Tax and Opposing Views

Amid continued citizen dissatisfaction over high housing costs, a plan to levy additional taxes on homes owned by the wealthy is also being pursued. A public hearing was held for the 'Pied-à-Terre' tax imposed on high-end apartments and homes owned by non-resident owners, pitting measures to mitigate wealth inequality against claims of property rights infringement.

Jennifer Gaboury, a member of the City University of New York faculty and staff union, argued for equity in sharing the burden of infrastructure maintenance costs.

So they're not paying for the infrastructure that supports and makes those apartments valuable. And we find that really unfair.

"So they're not paying for the infrastructure that supports and makes those apartments valuable. And we find that really unfair."

On the other hand, Jack Bollenbach, an owner of multiple properties, argued that excessive taxation would lead to poor apartment maintenance.

They don't understand the costs and the repair work. And then what happens is these apartments go down, they're not kept, and the people move out.

"They don't understand the costs and the repair work. And then what happens is these apartments go down, they're not kept, and the people move out."

Ongoing Pain Amid Soaring Living Costs

Amid housing supply shortages and upward pressure on rent, conflicts surrounding tenant protection and landlords' exercise of property rights are expected to continue for the time being.
