"Buying Time with Money": Pablo Air Chairman Kim Young-jun on Raising 107.5 Billion Won and Scaling Up

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 3 min read

Source: EO Korea · 8년 만에 1000억 투자받고 글로벌 진출하는 드론 스타트업 | 파블로항공 김영준. Summarised from that video.

Kim Young-jun, chairman of Pablo Air—which started with just three people in 2018 and has grown into a company employing around 270 staff with 107.5 billion won in cumulative investment raised—has revealed the story behind founding the business and his strategy for expansion.

Special Forces Experience and First Encounter with Drones

Kim enlisted in the Special Forces at age 20 and served for five years. He said he built endurance through limit-testing drills such as inland tactical training. After completing his military service in 2014, he witnessed the emergence of the recreational drone market, worked at a drone startup after returning to university, and began related research.

While watching a drone show of around 1,000 drones presented by an overseas company at the 2018 PyeongChang Winter Olympics, he decided to create drone shows using domestic technology. He founded Pablo Air after receiving 200 million won in seed funding from an angel investor who was his friend's father.

South Korea's First Successful Drone Show and Guinness World Records

In the early days of founding the company, Chairman Kim visited a drone show software developer in Latvia to learn their system and integrate it with the technology he was developing. In October 2018, he successfully staged South Korea's first drone show performance using 35 drones at Cheongna Lake Park in Incheon.

To differentiate from conventional drone shows focused mainly on lights, he subsequently planned drone pyrotechnic shows. Pablo Air set its first Guinness World Record by forming a corporate logo using 303 drones, followed by a 511-drone performance and breaking a third Guinness World Record in 2024.

"Once competitors emerged, I think capital strength became important. I have a philosophy that you buy time with money, and I naturally consider investors' money to be a debt."

Expansion into Aircraft Inspection and Defense Industry

To overcome revenue limitations of the drone show business, Chairman Kim broadened the application areas of his technology. A prime example is the aircraft exterior inspection project conducted with Korean Air for more than three years. The process—which previously required 8 to 10 people climbing ladders for 8 hours—was streamlined so that 4 drones and 2 operators complete it in just 30 minutes.

He also entered the defense market, which has grown in importance following the Russia-Ukraine war. To secure mass-production capabilities, the company merged with BLK, a defense firm with 43 years of history. Through this, it plans to significantly expand production capacity to 50,000 units this year, 100,000 units next year, and 200,000 units the year after next.

Attitude and Philosophy Required for Entrepreneurs

Chairman Kim cited people and personal networks as the most important elements in business. He explained that maintaining a contact list of over 10,000 contacts and building relationships where mutual help can be exchanged is where opportunities begin.

He also emphasized that entrepreneurship must be a long run rather than a short race. As business deals do not happen by being impatient, he advised that an attitude of moving forward steadily from a long-term perspective is necessary.
