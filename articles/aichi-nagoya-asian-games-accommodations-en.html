"Containers and Cruise Ships Instead of Apartments": The Frugal Athletes' Village for the Aichi-Nagoya Asian Games

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 3 min read

Source: KBS 세계는지금 · 아시안게임 D-7, 선수촌이 없다? (KBS_467회_2026.09.12.방송). Summarised from that video.

Unusual Athletes' Village of Containers and Large Cruise Ship

Ahead of the 20th Aichi-Nagoya Asian Games, temporary container accommodation facilities have been set up at the Garden Pier in Nagoya Port. The container athletes' village was introduced to reduce the burden of construction costs associated with building a new apartment-style athletes' village.

Each container accommodation unit is configured to house up to eight people. Insulation was reinforced to improve cooling efficiency, and space for large luggage was secured under the beds. A total of 2,000 athletes and team members will stay here across the facilities.

Yuta Matsuo, an official with the Aichi-Nagoya Asian Games Organising Committee, explained the purpose behind planning the container lodging.

国際総合競技大会の良さ、選手の皆様が国だとか宗教、慣習に関係なく集まれる拠点という形で、今回考えさせていただいたのがこちらのガーデンふ頭の移動式宿泊施設となっております。

"The charm of an international sports competition is that athletes can gather in one place regardless of nationality, religion, or customs. What we planned this time as a hub for interaction is this mobile accommodation facility at Garden Pier."

15,000 Athletes Distributed Across Accommodations... First Cruise Ship Deployment

In addition to the container athletes' village, a large cruise ship will be deployed as accommodation for athletes. This is the first time in the history of the Asian Games that a cruise ship is being used as an athletes' village. The large cruise ship will dock at Kinjo Pier in Nagoya Port to accommodate 4,000 athletes and officials.

The cost of chartering the cruise ship for 22 days is around 45 billion yen (approximately 400 billion won). The Organising Committee stated that this amount includes meals and services, significantly cutting the budget compared to the cost of constructing a new athletes' village.

The accommodation breakdown for the entire contingent of 15,000 people from 45 countries is as follows:

  • Container Athletes' Village: 2,000 people
  • Cruise Ship: 4,000 people
  • City Hotels: 9,000 people (distributed across around 50 hotels)

Reusing Existing Venues and Eco-Friendly Medal Production

Venue construction was also minimized. New construction was limited only to essential facilities, such as the main stadium for the opening and closing ceremonies and athletics events, as well as gyms for basketball and judo, while existing facilities will be used for the remaining sports. The scope of venue use extends beyond Nagoya to nearby Shizuoka and Osaka, and even includes venues located in Tokyo.

Frugality and eco-friendly elements were also reflected in the medals to be used in the games. Medals were produced using gold, silver, and bronze extracted from discarded electronic appliances collected from citizens across 27 regions, reaching the target amount of 1,495 kg early.

Over-Budget Concerns and Ticket Sales Challenges

Despite these cost-cutting efforts, total hosting costs are expected to exceed three times the original budget due to rising material costs and soaring labor expenses. Overall hosting costs are estimated at around 370 billion yen (approximately 3.2 trillion won).

Hideaki Omura, Governor of Aichi Prefecture, emphasized the significance of the games.

アジアの平和をね、この愛知・名古屋大会で確認したい。スポーツの力で確認したい、そんな思いです。

"I want to confirm peace in Asia through this Aichi-Nagoya Games. It is my desire to confirm it through the power of sports."

With the opening approaching, ticket sales have fallen short of expectations, making attracting spectators and generating excitement the Organising Committee's biggest challenge.
