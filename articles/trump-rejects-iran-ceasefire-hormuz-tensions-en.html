Trump Rejects Iran's '7-Day Ceasefire' Offer as Hormuz Conflict Drives Oil Above $100

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 5 min read

Source: KBS News · [이슈] 미, 이란 '7일 휴전안' 즉각 거부 "트럼프, 중간 선거 뒤 폭격 재개 가능성 언급"/2026년 9월 26일(토)/KBS. Summarised from that video.

US President Donald Trump has rejected a "7-day ceasefire plan" proposed by Iran and is maintaining a stance of military and economic pressure. Tensions in the Middle East are escalating further as Iran's blockade of the Strait of Hormuz and vessel attacks intersect with US sanctions against Iran.

Trump Rejects Iran's '7-Day Plan' Proposal, Mentions Possible Resumption of Bombing

According to foreign media reports and US officials, President Trump expressed skepticism about the ceasefire proposal presented by Iran and rejected the offer. Iran delivered the "7-Day Plan" to the US side, offering to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days on the condition that the US end its naval blockade and unfreeze Iranian assets.

However, President Trump reportedly assessed that Iran is unlikely to meet US demands and mentioned the possibility that bombing operations against Iran could resume after the upcoming November midterm elections.

US-Iran Indirect Contacts and Clash of Speeches at UN General Assembly

Dialogue and confrontation between the US and Iran unfolded simultaneously on the occasion of the UN General Assembly held at the UN Headquarters in New York. Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian expressed a willingness to engage in dialogue during his speech at the UN General Assembly, while maintaining a firm stance.

ما آماده گفتگو هستیم اما بدانند زبان زور را نخواهیم پذیرفت

"We are ready for dialogue, but they should know that we will not accept the language of force."

President Trump also sent a message of strong pressure to Iran through his speech at the UN General Assembly.

I will never allow Iran to have a nuclear weapon.

"I will never allow Iran to have a nuclear weapon."

Vessel Attacks in the Strait of Hormuz and Expansion of Maritime Control Zones

As retaliatory actions between the US and Iran intensify, civilian vessel damage continues to mount. Oil tankers and bulk carriers in the Strait of Hormuz and the Persian Gulf area have been struck by projectile and drone attacks, resulting in crew members being killed or missing.

As the US military and the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps continue strikes against opposing vessels, the Revolutionary Guard declared it would expand its maritime control zone to parts of the Gulf of Oman and the Arabian Sea. With fears of military conflict deepening, international oil prices surpassed $100 per barrel.

Tightening Aviation Sanctions Against Iran and Closing Air Routes

The US Department of the Treasury tightened sanctions against Iranian civilian airlines and warned that foreign entities cooperating with them would also be expelled from the dollar payment system. US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent specified that providing fuel or landing services to Iranian aircraft could make entities subject to sanctions.

Consequently, flights to and from Iran were suspended at major airports including Baghdad, Iraq, and Muscat, Oman, while major global airlines also took measures to suspend routes heading to Iran.
