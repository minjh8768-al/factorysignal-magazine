"Faltering Europe": From Euro Adoption Pains to Political Crises and Trade Conflicts

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 6 min read

Source: KBS 세계는지금 · 흔들리는 유럽 불안한 미래 (KBS_2026.08.01.방송). Summarised from that video.

The European continent is reeling from political uncertainty, high inflation, and trade conflicts. A series of disruptions is unfolding, ranging from monetary integration disputes in Eastern Europe and a resignation crisis facing the new British prime minister to restructuring in key German manufacturing sectors and large-scale protests by Western European farmers.

Bulgaria's Euro Adoption and Regime Collapse

Bulgaria converted its official currency to the euro on January 1, 2026. This measure was finalized after taking 19 years to meet the conditions for joining the eurozone. While the Bulgarian government expected eurozone integration to improve financial markets and drive economic development, opposition persisted, particularly among older adults and rural residents.

As public discontent over the rapid currency change and uncertainty escalated, large-scale nationwide protests erupted in December 2025. Ultimately, Prime Minister Rosen Zhelyazkov resigned, leading to political turmoil as the cabinet collapsed.

UK Controversies Over China Relations and Economic Challenges

In London, UK, protests were held against plans to build Europe's largest Chinese embassy on the site of the former Royal Mint. Located near London's financial district, the embassy site is ten times the size of the current embassy, raising concerns over espionage and the hacking of sensitive financial information.

UK Prime Minister Keir Starmer visited Beijing seeking to expand trade with China amid sluggish economic growth following Brexit. Domestically, however, public frustration reached a breaking point as the cost-of-living crisis deepened due to soaring energy bills and rising food prices after the Russia-Ukraine war.

Following the 2026 local elections, in which the hard-right Reform UK won the most seats and the ruling Labour Party suffered a crushing defeat, Prime Minister Starmer faced pressure to resign. Ed Davey, leader of the Liberal Democrats, formally raised a motion of no confidence against the government in a parliamentary speech.

The votes on this King's Speech ought to be interesting. A test of confidence in this government and this Prime Minister.

"The votes on this King's Speech ought to be interesting. A test of confidence in this government and this Prime Minister."

Kemi Badenoch, leader of the Conservative Party, also criticized the Prime Minister, stating that his ability to govern had reached its limit.

The Prime Minister is in office, but not in power. It is clear that he will not be able to deliver what little there is in this King's Speech, already run out of ideas and run out of road.

"The Prime Minister is in office, but not in power. It is clear that he will not be able to deliver what little there is in this King's Speech, already run out of ideas and run out of road."

In response, Prime Minister Keir Starmer maintained his stance that he would remain in power and pursue legislative reforms.

We are in government, and they are no longer even the opposition.

"We are in government, and they are no longer even the opposition."

Ukraine-Hungary Pipeline Dispute and Energy Security

Conflicts between Ukraine and Hungary escalated over the shutdown of the Druzhba oil pipeline running from Russia to Hungary. Ukraine cited facility damage from Russian attacks as the cause, but Hungarian Prime Minister Viktor Orbán countered that it was political blackmail.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy emphasized the urgent need for European Union support.

This is critical, critical for us. It's a resource to protect lives.

"This is critical, critical for us. It's a resource to protect lives."

On the other hand, Hungarian Prime Minister Viktor Orbán stated that without the resumption of oil supplies, Hungary would exercise its veto on loan support for Ukraine.

The Hungarian position is very simple. We are ready to support Ukraine when we get our oil, which is blocked by them. We are waiting for the oil.

"The Hungarian position is very simple. We are ready to support Ukraine when we get our oil, which is blocked by them. We are waiting for the oil."

German Manufacturing Slump and European Farmers' Protests

Germany's flagship steel and automotive industries are also suffering severe operational difficulties. Thyssenkrupp Steel has decided to cut 11,000 jobs—amounting to 40% of its total workforce—by 2030, while Volkswagen is also pursuing restructuring involving 50,000 employees. Rising energy costs, sluggish domestic demand in China, and the entry of Chinese electric vehicles are cited as key factors.

Meanwhile, farmers across Europe, including France, Germany, and Belgium, held large-scale tractor protests against the signing of the FTA between the EU and Mercosur. As the European Parliament passed a motion referring the legality of the agreement to the European Court of Justice for a ruling, the procedures for entering the FTA into force were completely suspended until a final verdict is reached.
