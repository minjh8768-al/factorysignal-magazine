US Treasury Yields Projected to See 5% Range 'New Normal' as Surge Hits South Korean Loan and Deposit Rates

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 4 min read

Source: SBS Biz 뉴스 · [📂금리 이슈 모아보기] 전쟁 끝나도 美 국채 안 돌아온다, 금리 브레이크 실종..한국도 ‘이자 폭탄’. Summarised from that video.

As US Treasury yields continue their high-altitude rally, analysis suggests that a high-interest-rate environment will become the 'new normal.' This ripple effect of high interest rates originating from the US is spreading to South Korea's financial markets, increasing the interest burden on households and businesses.

Surging US Treasury Yields and Caution Over Fed Tightening

As expectations for the resumption of negotiations between the US and Iran diluted and concerns over high oil prices and inflation persisted, selling pressure on US Treasuries reignited. The yield on 10-year US Treasuries briefly crossed 5.27%, a 19-year high, while the 30-year Treasury yield exceeded 5.58%, its highest level since June 2002. The 2-year Treasury yield also surged to 4.96%, nearing the 5% mark.

According to the CME FedWatch Tool, the probability of the US Federal Reserve raising its benchmark interest rate next month exceeded 70%. International credit rating agency Standard & Poor's (S&P) noted that Treasury yields could gradually rise as energy price shocks persist, while CNBC cited expert analysis indicating that even if war and oil price instability are resolved, long-term bond supply-demand imbalances make it highly likely for 10-year yields to remain around 5%.

Surge in Korean Treasury Yields and Forecasts of Mortgage Rates Reaching 8% Upper Limit

Driven by the surge in US Treasury yields, South Korea's Treasury yields rose across the board. The 3-year Treasury bond yield topped 4.1%, hitting a high, while yields on 2-year, 5-year, 10-year, 30-year, and 50-year bonds all rose. As a result, banks' funding costs have increased, adding upward pressure on lending rates.

Suh Ji-yong, a professor of business administration at Sangmyung University, explained the market situation as follows:

미 국채 금리 오르면서 우리나라 국고채 금리가 오르고 거기에 기인해서 회사채 금리나 금융채 금리가 오르면 은행들의 자금 조달 코스트가 올라가면서 대출금리 인상으로...

"As US Treasury yields rise, South Korea's Treasury yields go up, and as corporate and financial bond yields increase as a result, banks' funding costs rise, leading to higher loan rates..."

Fixed-rate mortgage yields, which use 5-year financial bonds as benchmarks, have seen their upper limit exceed 7% annually, with forecasts suggesting they could enter the 8% range in the future. Variable-rate mortgages are also facing upward pressure with a time lag due to rising COFIX.

Soaring Deposit Rates and Stock Outflows... Acceleration of Reverse Money Move

In addition to lending rates, bank deposit rates are also rising sharply. The average interest rate on one-year time deposits in the banking sector rose to 3.46% annually, with an increasing number of deposit products and banks offering top rates of 3.5% or higher per year. Led by regional banks, products offering over 3.8% annually increased, pushing the highest deposit rate up to 3.99% per year.

On the other hand, the stock market is facing yield pressure due to rising yields on safe-haven bonds. Daily average transaction value on the KOSPI has dropped significantly, and investor deposits are showing a downward trend. The balance of time deposits at the five major banks grew by about 75 trillion won over the five months since May, clearly highlighting a 'reverse money move' as funds shift from stocks into bank deposits.

Jang Keun-hyuk, a senior research fellow at the Korea Capital Market Institute, pointed out the background of stock market capital outflows as follows:

예금 금리를 1%p 올렸다고 해도 증시가 활황이면 또 이게 큰 영향이 되지는 않을 수 있는데 아무래도 코스피가 행보하는 부분이 있으니까 증시에서는 돈이 빠져나갈 요인이긴 하죠.

"Even if deposit rates are raised by 1 percentage point, it might not have a major impact if the stock market is booming. However, since the KOSPI is moving sideways, it is a factor driving money out of the stock market."

With the possibility of an additional base rate hike by the Bank of Korea within the year combining with the US Fed's stance of prolonged high interest rates, rising lending and deposit rates as well as expanding volatility in financial markets are expected to continue for the time being.
