'Deeply Disappointed During Injury'... How An Se-young's Outspoken Remarks Sparked Reform in Korean Sports

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 3 min read

Source: KBS News · [이슈] 22살 안세영이 쏘아올린 엄청 큰 공...대한민국 체육계 부조리 싹 바뀐 근황/2026년 10월 9일(금)/KBS. Summarised from that video.

Outspoken Remarks from the Top of the Olympic Podium

After reaching the top of women's singles badminton and winning the gold medal, An Se-young made outspoken remarks directed at the Badminton Korea Association immediately after the match. Instead of simply enjoying the joy of her glorious moment, she directly expressed her deep disappointment with the national team's injury management system and the association's complacent response.

In an interview, An candidly revealed the pain she felt during her injury treatment process and her disappointment with the association's administration.

처음에 오진이 있었던 순간부터 저는 계속해서 참고 경기를 하고 있었는데 부상을 겪는 상황과 그런 순간에 너무 많은 실망을 해서 그 순간을 잊을 수가 없거든요.

"From the moment there was an initial misdiagnosis, I kept persevering and competing through it. I experienced so much disappointment during that time while dealing with the injury that I can never forget those moments."

Exposure of Mandatory Gear and Outdated Customs

Following An's remarks, previously unknown absurdities in the badminton world came to light. It was revealed that athletes' freedom to choose equipment directly tied to performance had been restricted, such as regulations forcing them to wear shoes from national team sponsors, which caused severe blisters. An outdated culture was also exposed, in which she had to run personal errands—such as restringing seniors' rackets, cleaning rooms, and doing laundry—for a long period after being first selected for the national team in middle school.

While the association initially denied any conflict, an investigation by the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism confirmed the actual existence of several restrictive regulations, including mandatory use of national team sponsor equipment and restrictions on non-national team players participating in international competitions.

Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism Investigation and Systemic Reform

Triggered by An's exposure, the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism conducted an investigation into the Badminton Korea Association and recommended improving regulations that excessively restrict athletes' freedom to pursue their profession. The ministry entered consultations to quickly revise guidelines forcing the use of specific sponsor gear while taking existing sponsorship contracts into account, and moved to abolish restrictions on competing in international tournaments so that qualified players can compete even if they are not on the national team.

In addition, comprehensive reform measures were prepared, such as explicitly adding language to national team operational guidelines prohibiting seniors and coaches from ordering personal errands, and allowing individual equipment sponsorship contracts for athletes.

Shift Toward an Athlete-Centered System and Its Impact on Sports

Amid these changes, organizational overhaul got under way in earnest, including the replacement of the head of the Badminton Korea Association and the reorganization of the coaching staff. With an environment allowing her to fully focus on training and securing individual sponsorship contracts, An is enjoying her prime, winning the Asian Badminton Championships and continuing her Asian Games gold medal streak.

Observers evaluate that this served as a turning point not just for badminton, but for the sports sector as a whole, shifting governance from a results-oriented, coercive culture to one prioritizing athlete welfare and rights.
