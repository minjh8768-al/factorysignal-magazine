FOMC Holds Rates, Expert Advises Staying in Market Ahead of Potential Cuts Next Year

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 8 min read

Source: 김영익의 경제스쿨 · [긴급분석] FOMC 금리동결과 출렁이는 국내증시, 지금의 투자 전략은? | 김영익 교수. Summarised from that video.

At the July Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) meeting, the benchmark interest rate was frozen at 3.50-3.75%. Professor Kim Young-ik (김영익) analyzed the meeting results and the U.S. economic situation, emphasizing that despite high market volatility, investors should not leave the stock market from a long-term perspective.

FOMC July Meeting Results and Economic Diagnosis

At the July FOMC meeting, the federal funds rate was frozen at an annual 3.50-3.75% as expected by the market. However, three committee members who opposed the freeze advocated for an interest rate hike. The FOMC announced that economic growth is robust, productivity and capital investment are strong, and employment is increasing, but inflation has exceeded its target of 2%.

U.S. interest rates have shown flexibility, boldly lowering to 0% when the economy was poor and raising to 5.50% when conditions improved. During the COVID-19 pandemic, interest rates were cut to 0.25%, and over $4 trillion was released through quantitative easing. However, due to rising prices, the benchmark interest rate was increased, and quantitative tightening was implemented. Currently, interest rate hikes have stopped, and quantitative tightening has also paused.

The Present and Future of the U.S. Economy

The FOMC assessed the U.S. economy as robust. Following the pandemic, bold fiscal and monetary policies led to a rapid economic recovery, and it is currently growing at a potential growth rate of around 2%. Growth in the first quarter was 2.1%, and the market forecast for the second quarter is 2.3%. Employment has steadily increased, and although the unemployment rate has risen slightly from its low, it is stabilizing at a level lower than the historical average. Labor productivity, which increased by an annual average of 1.5% from 2008 to 2019, was assessed to have risen by about 2.2% since 2020.

Professor Kim Young-ik pointed out that inflation still significantly exceeds the Federal Reserve's (Fed) target of 2%. While some suggest raising the inflation target, Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh (케빈 워시) stated he would continue to adhere to the 2% target. Professor Kim Young-ik analyzed that the growth in U.S. consumption is likely to slow down. This is due to declining household savings rates, stagnant real household income, increased household interest burdens from higher interest rates, and weakening consumer sentiment. Consequently, he predicted that consumption would contract as early as the fourth quarter of this year, or by the first half of next year at the latest, leading to an overall slowdown in U.S. economic growth. Therefore, he assessed that the possibility of interest rate hikes is very low, and instead, there is a high likelihood of rate cuts next year.

Relationship with the Stock Market

The U.S. 10-year Treasury yield is currently maintaining a very high level, around 4.7%. In the long term, the 10-year Treasury yield is typically about 1.2 percentage points higher than the federal funds rate. In light of this, Professor Kim Young-ik expected the Fed to remain in a state where it can neither raise nor lower rates for the time being. However, he predicted that interest rates are likely to fall starting from the fourth quarter due to a consumption-led economic slowdown.

The correlation coefficient between the benchmark interest rate and the S&P 500 index has reached 0.73 since 2008. Professor Kim Young-ik explained that in the stock market, the economy and corporate profits are more important than interest rates, citing that the S&P 500 actually rose when benchmark rates increased and fell when benchmark rates decreased. However, the current S&P 500 index is overvalued at an all-time high, and the debate over an AI bubble continues. Professor Kim Young-ik predicted that U.S. stock prices are likely to see an adjustment because if consumption growth slows and the economy contracts next year, corporate profit growth will inevitably slow as well.

Wise Investment Strategy

Professor Kim Young-ik diagnosed that the current market is at an economic turning point, characterized by high volatility due to increasing uncertainty surrounding AI. He emphasized that precisely at such times, investors should not leave the stock market.

"장기적으로는 모든 자산 중에서도 주가 수익률이 제일 높거든요. 주식을 보면서 상당히 힘드시겠지만은 주식을 가지고 있으면서 경기도 판단하고 지금은 여유를 가지고 멀리 내다보셔야 될 것 같습니다."

"In the long term, stock returns are the highest among all assets. While it may be quite difficult to watch your stocks, you must hold them, assess the economy, and now, with patience, look far ahead."

Professor Kim Young-ik had advised increasing stock allocation until the first half of the year, but now he recommends reducing it, as leading indicators are likely to turn downward. He stated that if one's stock allocation is already high, it would be wise to reduce it during a short-term rebound and then seize the opportunity to buy cheaper later. Conversely, if one's cash allocation is high, short-term stock purchases might be possible, but he added that it would be better to acknowledge the trend has shifted to a downturn and wait a bit longer until economic indicators stabilize, capital inflows increase, and the AI bubble subsides.

In the long term, the stock market trends upwards, and the KOSPI has grown by an average of 8% annually since 2000. He emphasized that stocks do not always rise, so investors should maintain peace of mind, manage their health, and look far ahead. He advised that now is a time to prepare for another leap forward, and an opportunity to buy stocks cheaply will soon arise, urging investors to utilize this period to seize the chance to increase their wealth.
