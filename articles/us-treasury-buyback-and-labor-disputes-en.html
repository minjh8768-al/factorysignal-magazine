U.S. Bond Buybacks and Labor Disputes in Chips and Auto Sectors Highlight Divergent Market Views

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 4 min read

Source: 연합뉴스TV · [경제읽기] 현대차 노조, 전면 파업…2016년 이후 10년만 / 연합뉴스TV (YonhapnewsTV). Summarised from that video.

The U.S. Department of the Treasury announced a policy to buy back long-term Treasury bonds that it had issued, but reaction differed between the New York stock market and the Korean stock market. Kim Dae-ho (김대호), director of the Global Economic Research Institute, analyzed the background and limitations of the buyback policy, pointing out supply-demand instability and the fiscal deficit situation in the U.S. Treasury market.

Director Kim explained that in the New York stock market, concerns over a resurgence in Treasury yields and the fiscal burden on the U.S. Treasury played a major role, causing stocks to decline. In contrast, he assessed that in the Korean stock market, expectations regarding the U.S. long-term bond buyback announcement and treasury stock repurchases were relatively strongly reflected.

Semiconductor Companies' Shareholder Return Policies and Market Evaluation

There are differing perspectives among domestic and foreign investors regarding the scale of shareholder returns by major Korean semiconductor companies such as SK Hynix and Samsung Electronics. SK Hynix announced a shareholder return plan worth 40 trillion won, and Samsung Electronics is also expected to have a shareholder return scale exceeding 100 trillion won.

Director Kim presented cases of shareholder returns relative to cash flow in overseas companies, explaining that efforts by domestic companies to cancel treasury stock and expand dividends must be linked to earnings improvements from an industry recovery to serve as sustained stock price momentum.

미국 뉴욕증시에서는 바이백 소식에 증시가 환호하지 않았으나, 한국 증시에서는 자사주 소각과 주주환원 정책에 대한 기대감이 집중되며 반응이 엇갈렸다.

"In the U.S. New York stock market, the stock market did not cheer at the buyback news, but in the Korean stock market, expectations focused on treasury stock cancellations and shareholder return policies, resulting in mixed reactions."

Incentive Pay Controversy and Labor-Management Conflicts at Major Conglomerates

Labor-management conflicts surrounding the criteria and methods for paying incentive pay continue, centered on the semiconductor industry. Labor and management at SK Hynix reached a tentative collective bargaining agreement centered on a 6.3% wage increase and incentive pay distributed as 40% in cash and 60% in treasury shares.

On the other hand, at Samsung Electronics, controversies over equity between divisions arose, with union rallies held to protest the incentive pay gap between the semiconductor division and the finished products (DX) division. Director Kim analyzed that an incentive pay system linked to operating profit could act as a factor causing conflict within companies.

Hyundai Motor Union Strike and the Extension of Retirement Age Issue

The Hyundai Motor union went on a full-scale strike for the first time in 10 years since 2016, demanding changes to the bonus calculation standards and an extension of the retirement age. As the gap in perspectives between labor and management is large, reaching a negotiation settlement continues to face difficulties.

Director Kim noted that the extension of the retirement age is an issue directly tied to workers' job security and is an item with deep confrontation between labor and management. However, he emphasized that finding a point of agreement through dialogue is key, as there are past cases where Hyundai Motor's labor and management reached compromises even during strike situations.
