US Intensifies Airstrikes on Iran, Warning Military Pressure Will Continue Until Negotiations Begin

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 5 min read

Source: VOA 한국어 · VOA 한국어 뉴스 브리핑 7월 16일. Summarised from that video.

The U.S. military has escalated military pressure by intensifying airstrikes against Iran. Calling it a measure to weaken Iran's military capabilities that have been used to attack commercial vessels, the U.S. government stated it will continue the airstrikes until Iran agrees to negotiations.

US Intensifies Airstrikes on Iran, Warns of Expanding Targets to Power Facilities

U.S. Central Command announced that it conducted airstrikes to further degrade the Iranian military's ability to attack commercial vessels and civilian targets in the Strait of Hormuz and other areas. The strikes were carried out in a series of attacks lasting several hours, including daytime raids.

U.S. President Donald Trump stated that military attacks against Iran will continue until he deems them sufficient. He also warned that the next phase could expand target selection to include Iranian infrastructure such as power plants, bridges, and electrical facilities.

In contrast, Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps threatened in a statement that it would maintain the closure of the Strait of Hormuz and could also disrupt traffic in other crude oil export routes across the Middle East. Iran also launched missile and drone attacks targeting neighboring countries.

US Launches Diplomatic Campaign Against International Criminal Court

The U.S. government has launched a major diplomatic campaign against the International Criminal Court. The U.S. Department of State pointed out that it cannot tolerate actions by the International Criminal Court targeting U.S. service members and government officials or threatening American sovereignty.

In a video statement, the U.S. Secretary of State claimed that the International Criminal Court and its supporters are waging lawfare against the United States using the power of international law and treaties. The U.S. government urged other nations through consultations to reject the International Criminal Court's exercise of authority to prosecute American service members.

Rally Held Outside Chinese Embassy in Washington Demanding End to Forcible Repatriation of North Korean Defectors

Korean American youth, North Korean defectors, and North Korean human rights activists gathered outside the Chinese Embassy in Washington, D.C., to hold a rally demanding the Chinese government stop the forcible repatriation of North Korean defectors. Participants raised awareness about the dire situation faced by North Korean defectors in China and appealed for international attention.

China categorizes North Korean defectors not as refugees but as illegal economic migrants and continues its forcible repatriations. Human rights organizations and United Nations human rights bodies have pointed out that repatriated defectors face severe human rights violations, including torture, detention, and execution.

A human rights activist attending the rally emphasized China's compliance with international conventions.

First of all, they're signatory to the Refugee Convention. They've also signed the Convention Against Torture. Every single person who is forcibly repatriated is 100 percent tortured, 100 percent imprisoned, and in some cases executed.

"Many people have died. Sending them back to North Korea knowing what kind of punishment they will receive and how they are dying is, for a responsible nation, truly an act of driving them to their deaths."

North Korea Acquires Used Chinese Vessels, Suspected of Violating UN Security Council Sanctions

Signs have emerged that North Korea acquired four used vessels previously operated in China and registered them under its flag in the International Maritime Organization system. The identified vessels—Taesongsan 1, Rangrang 3, Haeyang 55, and Taeyangsan 1—were formerly operated under Chinese names before being changed to North Korean names and flags.

UN Security Council resolutions on North Korea prohibit the sale and transfer of new and used vessels to North Korea. Experts analyzed that North Korea, which faces difficulty acquiring vessels due to sanctions, continues to import used vessels from abroad, including China, to replace aging ships.
