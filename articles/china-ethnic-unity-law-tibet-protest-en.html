Tibetans and Minorities Push Back Against China's Ethnic Unity Law Amid Erasure Fears

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 4 min read

Source: KBS 세계는지금 · 중국 ‘민족단결법’ 시행, 티베트의 운명은? (KBS_463회_2026.08.15.방송). Summarised from that video.

As the Chinese government tightens control over ethnic minority areas and enforces the 'Ethnic Unity Law' justifying its assimilation policy centered on its own culture, backlash from minority communities, including Tibet, continues. With protest rallies and self-immolation incidents by exiled Tibetans overseas, international concern is mounting.

Self-Immolation in Front of UN Headquarters in New York and Provisions of the 'Ethnic Unity Law'

In front of the United Nations headquarters in New York, USA, Lobgyal Rangzen, an exiled Tibetan human rights activist, committed self-immolation while holding a Tibetan flag. Having fled to the United States 20 years ago and continued the Tibetan independence movement, he strongly criticized the Chinese Communist Party's Tibet policy in a last-will video left on the day of his self-immolation, saying it was driving residents to ruin.

China's Ethnic Unity Law, enacted before his self-immolation, mandates the use of standard Chinese (Putonghua) in minority schools and stipulates legal liability for acts inciting ethnic division or undermining unity abroad. In response, Kelsang Dolkar, an exiled Tibetan human rights activist in Australia, pointed out that the legislation poses a deep threat to exiles abroad.

And that is something very scaring and you know very intimidating for us, for someone like me as well who still have my family living in back in home in Tibet.

"The Ethnic Unity Law is a truly scary and intimidating law for us, especially for someone like me who still has family left behind in my hometown of Tibet."

Mandatory Standard Language Education Through Boarding Schools and Generational Disconnect

In places like Lhasa in the Tibet Autonomous Region of China, large boarding schools have been established to make minority students live in groups. Education that was previously conducted in Tibetan has all been switched to standard Chinese, and Tibetan can only be taken as a second language.

Regarding this Sinicization method of education, Zumretay Arkin, vice president of the World Uyghur Congress, raised concerns about the problem of generational disconnect caused by language control.

It creates a separation for generation. We see it very clearly with the young generation today where weave children have very much difficulty communicating even with their parents.

"The Ethnic Unity Law creates a generational disconnect. Among minority children, there are cases where they face difficulty even communicating with their own parents."

On the other hand, Einar Tangen of the Centre for International Governance Innovation mentioned the practical necessity of standard language education within Chinese society.

Who do not speak standard Putonghua would find it very difficult to move out of their locality. They would be denied jobs, they would be denied access to education.

"If you cannot speak standard Chinese in China, it is difficult to leave your local region. You could also be denied job or education opportunities."

Historical Conflict and Conflicting Positions

Since the Chinese People's Liberation Army occupied Tibet in October 1950, the Tibet Autonomous Region was established in 1965. The Chinese government has continuously emphasized ethnic unity through visits by President Xi Jinping and various events, and is making assimilation policies full-fledged, such as installing Mao Zedong statues in schools and removing Tibetan script from street signs.

The 14th Dalai Lama, the spiritual leader of Tibetans, has suggested the possibility of an agreement on the condition that cultural autonomy is guaranteed.

For preserving of Tibetan culture, Tibetan rich cultural heritage and very rich Buddhist tradition, they remain within the People's Republic of China.

"My position is that Tibet can remain part of the People's Republic of China as long as China promises to preserve Tibet's rich cultural heritage and rich Buddhist tradition."

However, Chinese Foreign Ministry Spokesperson Lin Jian dismissed the Dalai Lama's stance and maintained a tough position.

众所周知，十四世达赖并不是单纯的宗教人士，而是披着宗教外衣从事反华分裂活动的政治流亡者。

"The 14th Dalai Lama is not merely a religious figure, but merely a political exile engaging in anti-China separatist activities under the cloak of religion."

Assimilation Policies Expanding to Minorities Overall

China's ethnic assimilation policies are not limited to Tibet. In Hui Muslim residential areas in Yunnan Province, mosques have been remodeled into Chinese-style temples and Arabic signs have been removed, while Naadam, a traditional Mongolian festival, is also showing signs of Sinicization.

Under the massive language and cultural control pursued in the name of ethnic unity, concerns are rising among exiled communities worldwide, including the United States and India, that ethnic minorities' unique identity could disappear.
