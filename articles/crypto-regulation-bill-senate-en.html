Crypto Industry Urges Passage of Regulatory Bill, Calling Legislation a Rare Opportunity

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 4 min read

Source: CBS News · Coinbase vice chair says he's hopeful Senate will pass crypto regulation bill ahead of recess. Summarised from that video.

The U.S. Senate is under pressure to pass cryptocurrency regulation legislation ahead of its recess. The bill passed the House last year with bipartisan support and gives federal agencies new authority to monitor digital assets. A Coinbase vice president expressed optimism about the bill's passage.

Core of Crypto Regulation Bill 'Clarity Act'

As the Senate recess approaches, time is running short to consider key cryptocurrency regulation legislation known as the 'Clarity Act.' The bill passed the House of Representatives last year in a bipartisan vote. If passed, federal agencies will have new authority to monitor digital assets such as cryptocurrencies. An executive vice president at a company explained that the bill would help clarify the jurisdictions of two regulatory agencies: the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) and the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC).

The vice president noted that the classification issue between regulatory agencies is not a challenge unique to the crypto industry, pointing out that the two regulators have collaborated to determine asset classifications in the securities and commodities sectors as well. He noted that despite cryptocurrency existing for the past 15 years, there has been no clear definition of how this new technology fits into existing categories. He predicted that if the bill passes, investors and innovators will know who they need to interact with and what they need to disclose.

Industry Signals Willingness to Accept Regulation

The vice president emphasized that the crypto industry is not fighting against regulation, but rather "seeking" it. He called this phenomenon "unprecedented." Indeed, one crypto company even filed a lawsuit to compel the Securities and Exchange Commission to promulgate rules.

The industry is seeking to be regulated. It’s not fighting against it. In fact we had, Coinbase sued the SEC to try to get it to promulgate rules and regulations. Compared to the status quo, this bill is a rare no-brainer. This bill will finally provide comprehensive oversight in this space and the status quo provides none of those things. So if you vote against this bill, you are voting against hundreds of pages of protections and tools that this bill would otherwise provide.

Growing Political Spending and Its Background

According to a report by Public Citizen, the crypto industry spent $189 million in this midterm election cycle, an increase from the previous election cycle. This amount represents 37% of all tracked corporate political spending. In response, the vice president stated that he has no regrets about participating in the political process.

There's never going to be a regret to participate in the political process. Again, no different than any other company. What matters is not those contributions, but at the end of the day when we're talking about this bill, what matters is customers who are going to be protected and innovators who have the certainty to operate in this space.

He stated that the company is giving a voice to more than one in four Americans who hold cryptocurrency, and expressed pride in supporting Republican and Democratic lawmakers who recognize the potential of this technology and support comprehensive federal oversight.

Outlook for Passage of the Bill

The vice president noted that if the bill is not passed before the August recess, there is still a possibility it could be processed during a short session in September. He said that although there is time pressure and urgency, failing to finish the process right now does not mean there will be no time in September. He emphasized that crypto regulation remains one of the most bipartisan issues in Congress.
