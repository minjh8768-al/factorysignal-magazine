Paul Graham Says Today's Startups Take On Far Bigger Challenges Than Ever

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 5 min read

Source: Y Combinator · Paul Graham On Startups, Ambition, and Great Founders. Summarised from that video.

Paul Graham, founder of Y Combinator (YC), shared his thoughts on the essence of startups and the entrepreneurial environment in the AI era ahead of a lecture for YC's 47th batch. Graham emphasized that despite rapid changes in the technological landscape, the core factors determining a startup's success or failure remain the same as in the past.

"Startups Today Tackle Far More Serious Problems Than in the Past"

Graham assessed that startups recently backed by YC are addressing far more serious and monumental problems compared to the past. Unlike YC's early days, which focused mainly on commercial services or community platforms, recent batches feature an increasing number of entrepreneurs taking on unconventional fields such as developing cancer therapeutics or intercontinental ballistic missile technology for cargo transport.

You don't get much more serious than an intercontinental ballistic cargo.

"You can't get much more serious than intercontinental ballistic cargo."

He explained that as the startup ecosystem continues to grow, the depth and impact of the topics entrepreneurs tackle have expanded even further.

The Emergence of a New Google and Entrepreneurial Motivation

Graham pointed out that the 'frighteningly ambitious ideas' he had mentioned in the past are now becoming reality. He noted that replacing existing tech giants with entirely new paradigms, rather than launching direct attacks against them, is an effective strategy, citing OpenAI's shift of the search paradigm as a prime example.

He also analyzed that the real motivation driving entrepreneurs on a daily basis is the 'fear of failure' rather than the desire to get rich. He explained that staying intensely focused for over a decade to prevent servers from going down or the business from collapsing ultimately leads to the creation of immense value.

Changes in Cost Structure and Speed of Execution in the AI Era

He emphasized that despite changes in the development environment brought by advancements in AI technology, a startup's most critical metric for success remains 'speed of product launch.' This is because even as technical tools advance, differences remain in how quickly founders can conceive innovative ideas and introduce them to the market.

It used to be startups' big cost was salaries. And now suddenly you got to pay for GPUs.

"It used to be that a startup's biggest expense was labor costs, but now you suddenly have to pay for GPUs."

Graham noted that the most striking change with the emergence of AI startups is the shift in startup cost structures from being centered on labor costs to massive compute cost structures centered on GPUs and token usage fees.

The Concept of AGI and the Outlook for Future Founders

Regarding artificial general intelligence (AGI), Graham defined it not as a clear, single threshold, but as a process of a 'smear' with broad dimensions and breadth. He explained that this is demonstrated by the phenomenon where AI already surpasses humans in certain domains while still making errors on basic answers in others.

Lastly, regarding future startup founders, he predicted that the role will continue to be filled by human entrepreneurs possessing the strong execution capabilities and tenacity demonstrated over the past 20 years, rather than robots or AI.
