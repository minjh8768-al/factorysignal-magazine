Bank of Korea Raises Key Rate for First Time in 3 Years and 6 Months to Address Inflation, Exchange Rates, and Housing Prices

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 5 min read

Source: 경제 읽어주는 남자(김광석TV) · [속보] 한국은행, 3년 6개월만의 금리인상, 물가·환율 불안에 금리인상 단행 [즉시분석]. Summarised from that video.

First Key Rate Hike in 3 Years and 6 Months

The Bank of Korea decided to raise its key interest rate, shifting its monetary policy stance. This marks the first rate hike in three years and six months since the last rate increase in January 2023, breaking a 1-year and 2-month freeze. With this increase, the benchmark interest rate gap between South Korea and the United States narrowed from 1.25 percentage points to 1.00 percentage point.

Analysts evaluated the background behind the central bank's rate decision through three frameworks: price stability, financial stability, and economic assessment.

Rising Inflation Concerns and Import Price Pressures

The most prominent factor cited was elevated inflation. Import price growth, which serves as a leading indicator for South Korean inflation, remained high, continuously putting upward pressure on consumer prices. Consumer price inflation recently surged to 3.2%, while core inflation reached 2.5%, exceeding the price stability target.

Despite some stabilization in international oil and raw material prices, exchange rate instability has compounded import price burdens in Korean won terms. Consequently, the need to maintain a restrictive rate level to induce price stability has grown.

Exchange Rate Instability and M2 Money Supply Growth

On the financial stability front, external conditions such as the won-dollar exchange rate and M2 money supply growth served as key variables. As the won-dollar exchange rate hovered in the upper 1,400s to 1,500s level and stimulated import prices, foreign exchange authorities engaged in net selling responses, but a more structural monetary policy response was required.

South Korea's M2 money supply growth rate rose to 11.7%, surpassing the speed of money supply growth in the United States. As the money supply in circulation expanded rapidly, pressure on the won to depreciate mounted, prompting the need to moderate the pace of money supply growth through a rate hike.

Rising Seoul Apartment Prices and Household Debt Risks

For domestic financial stability conditions, real estate market overheating and the risk of rising household debt were taken into account. With apartment sales price indices in the capital region, particularly Seoul, showing strong upward trends, concerns were raised over an accelerating pace of household loan growth.

Alongside the government's moves toward tax reform and tighter regulations, this can be seen as reflecting the central bank's intention to restrict the inflow of funds into asset markets and foster financial stability through rate hikes.

Conditions for Rate Hike Supported by Solid Growth

In terms of economic assessment, the upward revision of the economic growth rate eased the burden of a rate hike. The Bank of Korea raised its GDP growth forecast for this year by 0.6 percentage points to 2.6%. Supported by strong semiconductor exports, the aggregate growth momentum of the South Korean economy remained solid, leading to the judgment that an excessive hit to the economy from the rate hike could be mitigated.

Regarding the future direction of monetary policy, expectations suggest that the Monetary Policy Committee will pause at one of its upcoming meetings before evaluating whether to pursue further rate increases.
