Won Plunges to 1,330 Level... Ryu Hyun-jung Sees Continued Downward Pressure from Massive Trade Surplus

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 3 min read

Source: SBS Biz 뉴스 · [이슈체크] 엔화 급등에 달러당 152엔…‘청산이 청산 부르는’ 연쇄 청산 우려. Summarised from that video.

The Korean won strengthened sharply against the U.S. dollar, driving the dollar-won exchange rate down to the 1,330-won range during intraday trading. The dollar-yen exchange rate, which had topped 160 yen, also dropped to the 152-yen level, showing a simultaneous strengthening of the yen. Ryu Hyun-jung (류현정), former head of the consumer banking group at Citibank, analyzed the supply-demand imbalance in the foreign exchange market and responses by key entities, offering an outlook on future exchange rates.

Exchange Rate Pulled Down by Massive Trade Surplus

Former Head Ryu cited a dollar supply surplus stemming from a massive trade surplus as the cause of the recent sharp drop in the dollar-won exchange rate. She analyzed that strong exports, centered on semiconductors, are continuing to supply dollars to the foreign exchange market.

대규모 무역흑자를 배경으로 한 달러화 공급 우위 상황이 달러 대 원화 환율을 가파르게 아래쪽으로 이끌고 있다.

"The situation of dollar supply dominance backed by a massive trade surplus is driving the dollar-won exchange rate sharply downward."

In fact, the trade surplus reached $34.75 billion in August, with the cumulative trade surplus from January to August reaching $202.5 billion. Former Head Ryu projected that downward pressure on the exchange rate could persist from a medium-term perspective as no factors absorbing dollars on the demand side have emerged.

National Pension Service's Dollar Purchases and Policy Shifts by Foreign Exchange Authorities

Shifts are also appearing in the policy stance of the National Pension Service and authorities, who had previously utilized currency hedging and foreign exchange swaps to curb high exchange rates. As the pace of the exchange rate drop accelerated, the National Pension Service turned to dollar purchases starting from the 1,380-1,390 won range.

Former Head Ryu assessed that while the National Pension Service's dollar repurchases could serve as a factor controlling the speed of the exchange rate decline in the short term, there are limits to absorbing the massive supply of dollars resulting from the large trade surplus.

'Super Weak Yen' Comes to a Pause... Rising Yen Value and Yen Carry Unwind Concerns

The dollar-yen exchange rate also dropped to the 152-yen level, driven by the aftermath of joint intervention by the U.S. and Japan, remarks by the U.S. Department of the Treasury regarding the yen's undervaluation, and expectations of an interest rate hike by the Bank of Japan. U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent pointed out the undervaluation of the yen and called on the Japanese side to establish sound monetary policy.

Regarding concerns over a yen carry trade unwind raised by some quarters, Former Head Ryu evaluated that it is not at a level to cause excessive anxiety in the market. The reason is that Japan's real interest rate remains at a negative level and its interest rates are low compared to other advanced economies, making it too early for the yen's strength to lock in as a dominant trend.

Cautions for Individual Currency Investors and Undisclosed Foreign Reserve Rankings

With the sharp drop in the exchange rate, dollar buying by individual investors is expanding, as dollar deposits at commercial banks have increased by the largest margin on record. However, Former Head Ryu advised a cautious approach, such as exchanging currency in installments, since room for further exchange rate declines remains given the supply-demand structure. For exporting companies, she viewed that the exchange rate drop would not lead to a fatal blow, as key sectors such as semiconductors have high operating profit margins.

Meanwhile, regarding the Bank of Korea switching the publication of national foreign exchange reserve rankings to non-disclosure when announcing foreign reserves for August ($442.3 billion), she noted that the Bank of Korea offered an explanation that ranking compilations are unrelated to a nation's external soundness and hold minimal analytical value.
