Crypto Market Trading Volume Plunges: A Phase of Liquidity Loss and Structural Decoupling, Not Just Price Drops

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 5 min read

Source: 더 리치 - 돈의 흐름을 읽어라 · "디지털 금은 어디로 갔나" 90% 폭락한 가상화폐 시장의 충격적인 몰락. Summarised from that video.

Sharp Drop in Trading Volume and Drying Liquidity in the Virtual Asset Market

In the virtual asset market, Bitcoin prices have dropped significantly from their peak, and media and public interest is cooling down. First-half trading volume at South Korea's five major won-denominated exchanges decreased by 54.6% year-on-year, dropping by more than half. The most threatening signal emerging in the market is not a simple price drop, but a disappearance of liquidity where trading dries up.

The situation is similar at major overseas exchanges. Coinbase, a major U.S. virtual asset exchange, announced plans to lay off about 700 employees, or roughly 14% of its total workforce, as its second-quarter revenue fell 19% year-on-year and it recorded losses for three consecutive quarters. According to CoinGecko data, global virtual asset market capitalization fell 12.6% during the second quarter to $2.1 trillion, while daily average trading volume decreased by 20.9%.

Structural Limitations and Oversupply of Altcoins

The sector hardest hit in this market slump is altcoins. Unlike stocks, bonds, or real estate, most altcoins fail to generate cash flows such as interest or rent, possessing a structural limitation of relying solely on the expectation that the next investor will buy at a higher price.

Continued new listings amid shrinking capital inflows exacerbated the supply-demand imbalance in the altcoin market. Over the past year and a half, a total of 274 new digital assets were listed on Upbit and Bithumb, major domestic exchanges. As a result, liquidity was diluted, causing the average price of the top five altcoins by market capitalization to drop about 60% from their all-time highs, with some altcoins once considered promising plummeting nearly 90%. The proportion of Binance-listed altcoins trading above their 200-day moving average also shrank to the 3% level.

Strong Performance of Real-World Asset Tokenization (RWA) and Stablecoins

Even as overall trading volume in the virtual asset market dried up, the market for blockchain-based real-world asset (RWA) tokenization and stablecoins showed growth. According to Binance Research data, the global RWA tokenization market size surged from about $21.5 billion early this year to about $31.4 billion as of May.

In particular, the size of tokenized assets linked to U.S. Treasuries exceeded $15 billion. Tokenized funds issued by major Wall Street asset managers, such as BlackRock's "BUIDL" and Franklin Templeton's "FOBXX," led the market, accounting for more than half of all Treasury-linked assets. Furthermore, the total market capitalization of stablecoins pegged 1-to-1 with the U.S. dollar maintained the $305.1 billion level despite the broader market decline, strengthening their position as capital reserves and payment infrastructure.

Re-evaluation of Bitcoin and Key Points to Watch Ahead

Unlike gold, which broke all-time highs amid geopolitical crises, Bitcoin showed a downward trend from its peak, tracing a trajectory different from traditional "digital gold" or a safe-haven asset. Bitcoin is evaluated as having moved closer to a liquidity indicator that responds first to market money supply and risk appetite rather than a safe-haven asset.

However, Bitcoin is distinguished from most altcoins in that it has no issuer, its total supply is fixed at 21 million, and a connection path to institutional finance is secured through spot ETFs. Key indicators for judging the future market direction include whether total stablecoin supply remains solid above $300 billion, weekly capital inflow trends into spot Bitcoin ETFs, and the legislative progress of domestic and international won-denominated stablecoin and virtual asset regulatory bills.
