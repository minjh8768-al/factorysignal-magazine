KRW Stablecoin Market Opens Through Institutionalization: 3 Key Points to Watch

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 4 min read

Source: MBS COIN NEWS · 원화 스테이블코인, 소문이 아닌 제도가 만드는 시장이 온다. Summarised from that video.

Explosive Growth of Global Stablecoin Market and US Dollar Hegemony

Global stablecoin market capitalization is growing rapidly, surpassing approximately $320 billion (approx. 400 trillion won). This scale exceeds the foreign exchange reserves of 95 countries worldwide, and Standard Chartered projected that the market size could reach $2 trillion (approx. 2,600 trillion won) by the end of 2028. The U.S. Treasury Secretary also stated that the $2 trillion projection is reasonable, while Bernstein saw that it could expand to up to $4 trillion by 2035.

Behind the U.S. push to foster the stablecoin market lie securing demand for short-term Treasury bonds and the digital expansion of dollar hegemony. The "GENIUS Act," enacted in July 2025, mandated that 100% of the issuance amount be backed by safe assets such as cash and U.S. short-term Treasury bonds with maturities of 93 days or less. Consequently, a structure is formed in which demand for U.S. short-term Treasury bonds surges as stablecoin issuance increases.

Implementation of the US GENIUS Act and Specific Legislative Schedule

Enforcement rules for the U.S. GENIUS Act must be prepared by July 18, 2026, one year from the date of the law's enactment, and the law itself is scheduled to take effect on January 18, 2027, or 120 days after the release of the final rule, whichever is earlier. The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) has already completed gathering public comments on proposed rules containing capital, reserve, and custody requirements, and agencies under the Department of the Treasury have also announced proposed rules related to anti-money laundering, entering the final stage of institutionalization.

Current Status of Domestic KRW Stablecoin Institutionalization Efforts

As concerns grow that borderless dollar coins could shrink the standing of the Korean won if they penetrate the domestic payment market due to complete U.S. regulations, the South Korean government and the Bank of Korea are also accelerating their efforts. Through its 2026 Economic Growth Strategy, the government formalized plans to push for the enactment of the Digital Asset Basic Act and the introduction of a spot Bitcoin ETF in the second half of this year. The Governor of the Bank of Korea also urged prompt legislation in a business report to the National Assembly, expressing an open stance recognizing the competitive and complementary relationship between Central Bank Digital Currency (CBDC) and private stablecoins.

Currently, the key issue in domestic institutionalization is the shareholding structure of issuers. The Bank of Korea and financial authorities have discussed allowing issuance starting from consortia in which banks hold a "50% + 1 share" stake or more, while a compromise proposal combining a bank "50% + 1 share" stake with around 34% fintech equity is also being presented in the National Assembly.

Realignments Among Financial Sector, Fintechs, and Exchanges, and Investor Precautions

Even before regulations are finalized, moves to combine bank credibility, fintech payment touchpoints, and virtual asset exchange liquidity are materializing in the market. Hana Bank is pursuing the acquisition of a stake in Dunamu ahead of its merger with Naver, seeking to combine foreign exchange networks with Upbit's liquidity and Naver Pay's payment infrastructure, while Kakao Pay, KB, and Toss are also working on alliance plans between banks and fintechs.

Experts highlight three checkpoints for investing. The first is the ban on interest payments and the strictness of reserve standards in the final rules of the U.S. GENIUS Act, the second is the official introduction of the second-phase Digital Asset Bill in the National Assembly, and the third is the issuer shareholding ratio guidelines to be announced by financial authorities in the second half of the year. On the other hand, they advised caution against chasing stocks that surge merely on rumors of trademark filings or memorandum of understanding (MOU) signings, or using excessive leverage shaken by regulatory headline news, as these increase the risk of liquidation.
