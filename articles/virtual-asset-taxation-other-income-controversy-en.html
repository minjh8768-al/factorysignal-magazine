Taxing Crypto as 'Other Income' Off-Track with Global Trends, Driving Demands for Tax Reform

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 3 min read

Source: 서울경제TV · 코인 과세, 투자자들이 분노하는 진짜 이유...진짜 문제는 세율이 아닙니다. Summarised from that video.

Ahead of the government's implementation of virtual asset taxation, controversy continues over taxation methods and income classification standards. The current bill focuses on classifying capital gains from virtual assets as other income, applying a 22% tax rate after a deduction of 2.5 million won.

Author Kim Chang-ik (김창익) pointed out that there is a fundamental problem with the South Korean government's perspective on virtual assets. He explained that classifying income earned through virtual asset investment as other income—such as lottery winnings or lecture fees—rather than financial investment income does not align with global market trends.

정부가 가상자산을 기타자산으로 보고 있다는 것이 가장 큰 문제다. 비트코인을 팔아서 돈을 버는 것을 복권 당첨된 것과 똑같이 보는 것이다.

"The biggest problem is that the government views virtual assets as 'other assets.' Making money by selling Bitcoin is treated the same as winning the lottery."

Major Countries Reclassifying Crypto as Financial Investment Income… Japan Also Pursuing Tax Reform

Kim explained that major overseas countries, including the United States, Europe, the United Kingdom, and Japan, are reclassifying virtual assets as financial investment income and updating their relevant tax systems. The direction is to recognize virtual assets as financial investment products rather than one-off income assets.

In particular, Japan previously classified crypto as miscellaneous income subject to heavy taxation of up to 55% under comprehensive income tax, but recently recognized the issue and began reforming the system. According to the published Liberal Democratic Party guidelines and tax reform proposal, Japan is pursuing a plan to reclassify virtual assets as financial investment products following a grace period and lower the tax rate to around 20%.

Recycling a Four-Year-Old Bill… Criticism Over Failure to Reflect Market Changes

Critics also pointed out that domestic tax policy fails to properly reflect market changes and global trends, noting that while the implementation of taxation was postponed, no active supplementation or modification of the bill's contents was made.

Kim criticized the practice of repeatedly postponing the income tax amendment drafted in the past, only to pull it out and apply it as is without content revisions. He stated that while major countries such as the United States are updating their systems by treating virtual assets from the perspective of national strategy and security assets, domestic policy remains stuck in a framework from years ago.
