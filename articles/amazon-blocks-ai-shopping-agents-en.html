Amazon Blocks AI Shopping Agents... "Protecting Ad Revenue and Customer Data"

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 4 min read

Source: 티타임즈TV · 메타 뮤즈를 왜 아마존은 막고 쇼피파이는 열었나?. Summarised from that video.

As Meta's unveiled personal AI agent 'Muse' draws significant attention, major e-commerce companies including Amazon are blocking access to external AI agents. In contrast, Shopify and Walmart are taking a contrasting approach by collaborating with Meta and actively participating in the AI agent ecosystem.

The Real Reason Beyond Security Concerns... Protecting 'Ad Revenue'

In an official statement, Amazon stated its reasons for the block, saying Muse accessed its system without prior agreement, failed to properly identify itself as an AI agent, and handled login information in a risky manner. However, the industry interprets this as a move to protect Amazon's core revenue stream, its advertising business, beyond mere technical and security issues.

Amazon's total advertising service revenue in 2025 reaches approximately $68.6 billion (about 92 trillion KRW), and according to market research firm eMarketer, about two-thirds of this comes from sponsored ads displayed in search results on the site. While ad exposure opportunities arise as consumers directly search for and compare products, if AI agents perform the search and comparison instead, sponsored ad exposure opportunities will plummet.

Concerns Over Losing Customer Touchpoints and Context Data

Besides the reduction in ad exposure, the accumulation of consumer preferences and purchase context data on external AI platforms is also a threat to Amazon. This is because preference information shared during conversations between consumers and external AI remains with platforms like Meta, leaving Amazon with only the final order information.

Amazon seeks to maintain control over its services—from product recommendations to purchases and ad links—through its own shopping AI, 'Alexa for Shopping'. On the other hand, since 76% of Shopify's revenue comes from merchant solutions such as payment processing and store operation fees, it values the occurrence of purchases through its own payment solution itself more than the path consumers took to discover products, thus welcoming external AI agents.

Opening Doors to External AI in the Seller Realm

Interestingly, while Amazon blocks external AI at consumer touchpoints, it actively allows connections to external AI such as Claude in 'Seller Central', its service for sellers.

Amazon unveiled a plugin that allows sellers to check inventory, forecast sales, adjust prices, and generate product descriptions through Claude conversations without logging into their management screen each time. Furthermore, it announced a feature to connect accounts from other platforms such as eBay, Shopify, TikTok, and Walmart to Seller Central, enabling them to manage multi-channel sales and orders in one place.

The Battle for Supremacy Among Shopping Platforms and Future Outlook

Y Combinator co-founder Paul Graham evaluated Amazon's decision to block agents as follows:

AI 에이전트를 차단하는 기업은 스타트업에 경쟁 서비스를 만들 기회를 열어주는 셈입니다.

"Companies that block AI agents are essentially opening up opportunities for startups to create competing services."

As starting points for shopping diversify into Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, and AI agents, Amazon is responding to market changes by maintaining initiative over consumer touchpoints while expanding Seller Central to broaden its business scope into logistics and order management for transactions on other platforms.
