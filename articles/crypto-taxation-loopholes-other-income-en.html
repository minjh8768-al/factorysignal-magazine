"Treating Crypto Like Lottery Winnings?": Flaws Pointed Out in 2027 Coin Taxation Scheme's 'Other Income' Classification

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 3 min read

Source: 서울경제TV · 코인이 복권 당첨? 2027년 가상자산 과세의 치명적인 허점 [김창익 작가]. Summarised from that video.

As the government prepares to implement taxation on virtual asset capital gains starting in 2027, criticism is being raised regarding fundamental flaws and problems in the tax system.

Crypto Asset Income Classified as 'Other Income,' Not Financial Investment

According to the government's Income Tax Act amendment, virtual asset capital gains are classified as "other income" rather than financial investment income. Accordingly, a tax rate of 22% will be applied to capital gains exceeding an annual deduction of 2.5 million won.

Author Kim Chang-ik (김창익) pointed out on air that the government's perspective on virtual assets itself is the biggest problem. He explained that profits from virtual asset investments are being defined as one-off other income, similar to lottery winnings or artwork sales.

비트코인을 팔아서 돈을 버는 것을 복권 당첨된 것하고 똑같이 보는 것이다.

"Making money by selling Bitcoin is treated the exact same as winning the lottery."

Loss Carryforward and Loss Offsetting Impossible... Concerns Over Investor Disadvantages

If virtual asset income is classified as other income, investors face critical disadvantages. This is because, unlike financial investment income, loss carryforward deductions and tax-loss harvesting do not apply.

For example, if an investor incurs a loss of 50 million won in 2027 and makes a profit of 50 million won in 2028, the actual cumulative profit is 0 won, yet a situation arises where 22% tax must be paid on the 50 million won earned in 2028. Additionally, even if losses occur in other financial assets such as the stock market, offsetting those losses against virtual asset gains is impossible.

Criticism over Bucking Global Trends and Administrative Expediency

Author Kim Chang-ik assessed that South Korea's policy conflicts with global trends in which major nations, such as the United States, Europe, and Japan, recognize virtual assets as financial investment assets and revise related tax systems. In the case of Japan, moving away from its past method of levying up to 55% comprehensive tax under miscellaneous income, it is promoting tax reform to classify them as financial investment products from 2028 and apply a 20% tax rate.

On the other hand, he criticized the South Korean government's administrative expediency in repeatedly postponing the Income Tax Act amendment prepared in 2022 and now attempting to apply it as is, without properly reflecting market changes or expert feedback.
