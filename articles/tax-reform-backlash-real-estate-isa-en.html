Backlash Mounts Over Real Estate and Financial Tax Reforms Amid Warnings of Eroding Trust

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 4 min read

Source: YTN · [스타트 경제] 정부, 'ISA·주가누르기' 재검토 착수...부동산 세제까지 '재검토' / YTN. Summarised from that video.

Flood of Legislative Opinions on Real Estate Tax Reform Proposal

Following the government's announcement of tax reform plans, controversy surrounding real estate tax and Individual Savings Account (ISA) revisions is spreading. Thousands of public legislative opinions were submitted regarding amendments to the Comprehensive Real Estate Tax Act and the Income Tax Act, sparking backlash over tougher taxation on non-resident single-home owners and the scope of recognized actual residency requirements.

Seok Byung-hoon, an economics professor at Ewha Womans University, pointed out flaws in the policy design during a broadcast interview, noting strong calls to recognize exceptions to actual residency due to various circumstances, such as grandparents caring for grandchildren or remodeling work.

Listings Rise but Transactions Vanish... Concerns Over Market for Cash-Rich Buyers Only

Since the tax reform announcement, apartment listings have increased, particularly in Seoul's Gangnam and Seocho districts, but actual transactions are not taking place due to loan regulations. Professor Seok evaluated that for high-priced homes valued at over 4 billion won, mortgage limits are restricted, making it difficult for anyone other than cash-rich individuals to buy.

주택을 팔아야 하는 고령 은퇴자들은 대출 규제 때문에 현금 부자가 아니고서는 집을 살 수 없는 상황에 직면해 있다. 결국 현금 부자들만의 거래가 될 위험이 크다.

"Elderly retirees who need to sell their homes are facing a situation where no one except cash-rich individuals can buy homes due to loan regulations. Ultimately, there is a high risk that transactions will become exclusive to the cash-rich."

Spillover Effects Spreading to Rental Market

Anxiety is also growing in the rental market as landlords choose to move into their properties themselves due to stricter taxation on non-resident single-home owners and limits on special deductions for long-term holding. Professor Seok expressed concern that a sharp drop in the supply of rental housing in key areas could trigger a chain reaction, pushing tenants to outer suburbs.

ISA Reform Proposal and Policy Trust Issues

In the financial market as well, controversies arose over reduced benefits in ISAs, such as the elimination of rolling over unused contribution limits and restrictions on maturity extensions. Consequently, moves are underway to formulate supplementary measures following the president's instruction for a complete review.

Professor Seok noted that if dynamic inconsistency repeats—where policies are announced without thorough review, applied retroactively, or frequently reversed—damage to end-users and financial consumers will inevitably continue.
