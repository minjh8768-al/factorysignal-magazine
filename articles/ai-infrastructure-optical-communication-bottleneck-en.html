Connectivity Emerges as AI Data Centers' New Bottleneck: An Analysis of the Optical Communications Ecosystem

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 5 min read

Source: 티타임즈TV · 엔비디아, 브로드컴, TSMC가 칩 속에 광케이블을 심는 이유. Summarised from that video.

Following GPUs, memory, power, and cooling, "connectivity"—which handles data transmission—is emerging as a new bottleneck across AI infrastructure. Due to the nature of AI data centers, where tens of thousands of GPUs must operate like a single giant computer, any blockage in data exchange paths degrades overall system performance.

Limits of Copper Wiring and the Emergence of Optical Communications

In traditional data centers, copper wiring has been primarily used for short-range connections between servers. However, with the explosive surge in the number of GPUs and the rapid increase in data transmission per port, copper wiring faces signal attenuation and heat dissipation issues.

While high-speed electrical signals drop to one-hundredth of their strength after traveling just 15cm along a copper wire, optical fibers retain half their signal strength even after 15km. In addition, optical communications consume less power, generate less heat, and offer an overwhelming advantage in bandwidth by sending light of different wavelengths simultaneously through a single strand.

Optical Communication Technology Penetrating from Outside to Inside

The adoption of optical communications in AI data centers is taking place in stages. The first stage involves connections between racks, where replacing connections between leaf switches and spine switches with optical cables and optical modules is currently expanding in earnest.

The second stage is co-packaged optics (CPO) technology, which reduces full-path losses inside switches. By integrating optical conversion components directly inside the package next to the switch chip rather than at external ports, the travel distance of electrical signals is reduced from the previous 30cm to a few millimeters. This lowers power consumption per port from 30W to around 9W and significantly reduces signal loss.

The final stage is optical I/O technology, which processes the input and output of the GPU chip itself using light. Because this requires sweeping changes from chip design to packaging, it is expected to be the last to transition, but it is considered a technology that will fundamentally alter how GPUs are deployed and operated.

China's Volume vs. US and Taiwan's Technological Tug-of-War

Roles within the optical communication industry ecosystem are divided by country. Mass production and assembly of finished optical modules are led by Chinese companies, with global No. 1 company InnoLight supplying large volumes of optical modules to US Big Tech firms.

On the other hand, US companies Lumentum and Coherent dominate the market for lasers—the core component inside optical modules—while US-based Corning dominates the optical fiber market. Forecasts suggest that as CPO technology adoption accelerates in the future, leadership could shift away from China, which handles simple assembly, toward US firms with chip design capabilities such as Nvidia, Broadcom, and Marvell, as well as Taiwan's TSMC, which possesses packaging technology.

However, the CPO market is still in its infancy, and traditional optical module methods will continue to be used for long-distance segments, meaning the battle for leadership between China, holding production volume, and the US, holding core technology, is likely to persist for years to come.
