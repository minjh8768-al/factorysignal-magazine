Will Crypto Taxation Be Postponed for Another 3 Years? "Institutional Infrastructure Must Come First"

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 3 min read

Source: 한국경제TV · 가상자산 과세 2030년까지 또 유예?…왜 계속 미루나ㅣ진현수 변호사ㅣ크립토랩스 Cryptolabs. Summarised from that video.

Controversy surrounding the taxation of virtual asset investment income continues as an amendment to the Income Tax Act is pursued to push back the tax, which was scheduled to take effect next year, by three more years.

Background and Issues Surrounding the Proposed 3-Year Tax Postponement

Taxation on virtual asset investment income was originally scheduled to take effect in 2022, but was repeatedly postponed to 2023, 2025, and 2027. Under the current amendment bill, a plan is being pursued to delay implementation once again to January 1, 2030.

Attorney Jin Hyun-soo (진현수) of Decent Law Firm pointed to the lack of legal framework and infrastructure related to virtual assets as a major background reason for pushing for the tax postponement.

디지털자산 기본법으로 가상자산의 법적 성격을 명확히 해야 과세 방식도 정할 수 있습니다. 과세 인프라가 미비한 상태에서 과세를 강행하게 되면 투자자만 부담을 지게 됩니다.

"Only when the legal nature of virtual assets is clarified through the Basic Act on Digital Assets can the taxation method also be determined. If taxation is forced through while the tax infrastructure is inadequate, the burden will fall solely on investors."

Institutional Requirements to Be Met Before Implementing Taxation

Attorney Jin outlined three requirements that must be met before full-scale implementation of taxation.

  • Clarifying the legal nature of virtual assets through the enactment of the Basic Act on Digital Assets
  • Establishing investor protection measures on par with capital markets, such as regulating unfair trade practices and protecting user deposits
  • Building tax infrastructure at the National Tax Service to handle acquisition price calculations and aggregate transaction history across multiple exchanges

He also pointed out that forcing taxation forward under inadequate institutional conditions could significantly increase legal disputes, such as tax appeals.

Preparations for Investors and Businesses When Taxation Becomes Reality

Transactions on overseas exchanges, decentralized exchanges (DEXs), and on-chain transfers between personal wallets present limitations because tax authorities have difficulty tracking details, relying instead on voluntary reporting by individuals.

In response, Attorney Jin advised that investors would do well to organize acquisition dates, prices, and transfer records between exchanges in advance. He also emphasized that virtual asset service providers must prepare by standardizing transaction data in line with the expansion of the Travel Rule and storing it in a format capable of being submitted to the National Tax Service.
