Korean Won Stablecoin Bill Stalls for Over a Year Amid Clashes Over Issuers and Ownership Limits

FSFactory Magazine 팀 · 3 min read

Source: 김경제 : Economics · 원화 스테이블코인, 우리는 왜 아직도 없을까? 미국은 이미 법까지 만들었다. Summarised from that video.

While major countries including the United States are accelerating the institutionalisation of stablecoins, the Framework Act on Digital Assets containing the institutionalisation of Korean won-based stablecoins has reportedly been tied up in South Korea's National Assembly for over a year.

Concept of Stablecoins and the Legislative Gap Between the US and South Korea

Stablecoins are cryptocurrencies designed to reduce price volatility by pegging their value 1-to-1 to fiat currencies such as the US dollar or Korean won. Because they enable fast, 24-hour transfers and payments without going through banks, they are drawing attention as a payment method in cross-border transactions.

The United States is leading the race for institutionalisation, having passed relevant legislation such as the GENIUS Act. The industry expects the expansion of dollar-based stablecoins to occur rapidly once the law takes effect. In contrast, South Korea's Framework Act on Digital Assets, which includes the institutionalisation of Korean won stablecoins, has failed to pass for over a year since its introduction to the National Assembly.

Fierce Debate Over Issuers of Korean Won Stablecoins

The biggest reason for the legislative delay is disagreement over "who can issue Korean won stablecoins."

  • Bank of Korea and Banking Sector Position: They maintain that strictly regulated banks must lead to preserve the stability of the overall financial system. Their position is that issuance authority should only be granted to consortia in which banks hold at least a 51% stake.
  • Fintech and Virtual Asset Industry Position: They push back, arguing that regulation centered solely on banks could slow down the pace of innovation. They contend that fintech companies and virtual asset exchanges that already possess technology and user bases should also be allowed to participate in issuance.

Discussions Delayed Amid Local Elections and Political Schedules

The level of ownership restrictions on major virtual asset exchanges is also one of the key issues. Opinions are divided among government ministries and stakeholders over whether to apply ownership limits of 15% to 20%.

In addition, political issues such as local election schedules overlapped, leaving the National Assembly with insufficient bandwidth to focus on reviewing the bill, which is analyzed as another major reason for the legislative delay.

Impact on the Domestic Market and Future Legislative Schedule

Experts point out that if the domestic payment market loses dominance to dollar-based stablecoins due to the absence of a Korean won stablecoin bill, it could affect monetary sovereignty in the long term.

The government and the ruling party plan to operate an in-party digital asset task force to coordinate details, introduce a consolidated bill during the regular National Assembly session in September, and push for its passage within the year. Once the bill passes, a restructuring of the virtual asset market is expected to follow, including the delisting of coins that fail to meet qualification requirements.
